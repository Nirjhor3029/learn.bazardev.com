## 2. One trap per chapter — the master list

| # | Topic | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| [1](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/01-how-ielts-reading-is-scored) | Scoring/overview | Scanning for exact question words only. | Scan for **meaning** — the passage almost always paraphrases. |
| [2](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/02-true-false-not-given) | True/False/Not Given | Treating "Not Given" as "probably false." | **False** means the passage contradicts it; **Not Given** means the passage is silent on it. |
| [3](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/03-matching-headings) | Matching headings | Picking a heading that matches one detail in the paragraph. | Match the paragraph's **main idea**, not a supporting detail (a distractor). |
| [4](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/04-matching-information-and-features) | Matching information/features | Assuming these questions follow the passage's paragraph order. | Matching information/features questions are often **not** in passage order — check each one independently. |
| [5](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/05-multiple-choice-questions) | Multiple choice | Picking the option with the most "borrowed" exact words from the passage. | **Predict** the answer before reading the options — borrowed words are often distractors. |
| [6](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/06-sentence-and-summary-completion) | Sentence/summary completion | Writing a correct answer that exceeds the word limit. | Exceeding the limit scores **zero**, however correct the meaning. |
| [7](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/07-table-flow-chart-and-diagram-completion) | Table/flow-chart/diagram | Ignoring what the column header predicts about the missing word's type. | Let headers and sequencing words **narrow the search** before you read closely. |
| [8](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/08-short-answer-questions) | Short-answer questions | Paraphrasing your own answer instead of copying the passage's exact wording. | Copy the exact word/phrase — these answers are rarely paraphrased. |
| [9](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/09-skimming-and-scanning) | Skimming and scanning | Reading the whole passage word by word from the start. | Skim for structure first, then scan for the specific fact each question needs. |
| [10](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/10-guessing-unknown-words-from-context) | Unknown words | Stopping and panicking at every unfamiliar word. | Only dig for meaning when a question depends on that exact word. |
| [11](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/11-spotting-paraphrase) | Spotting paraphrase | Assuming shared vocabulary between question and passage means a statement is True. | Matching words isn't matching meaning — check the full sense agrees. |

## 3. The combined pre-test checklist

**Before you start each passage:**
1. Did you **skim** the passage first to build a paragraph map (Chapter 9)?
2. Did you note the **word limit** for each completion/short-answer section (Chapter 6, 8)?

**While answering:**
3. Are you scanning for **paraphrases and synonyms**, not just exact words (Chapter 11)?
4. For True/False/Not Given: is the answer **False** (contradicted) or **Not Given** (silent) — not confused with each other (Chapter 2)?
5. For matching headings: does your choice match the **main idea**, not a detail (Chapter 3)?
6. For multiple choice: did you **predict** before reading the options (Chapter 5)?
7. For any completion type: does your answer fit the **word limit and grammar** of the gap (Chapter 6, 7)?
8. For short-answer questions: is your answer **copied exactly** from the passage, spelling included (Chapter 8)?

**If you hit an unknown word:**
9. Does the question actually depend on this word? If not, skip it and move on (Chapter 10).

## 4. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Which single reading mistake underlies almost every question type in this course?</strong></summary>

**Scanning for exact words instead of meaning** — nearly every question type depends on recognising paraphrase.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. A statement contradicts the passage directly. Is it False or Not Given?</strong></summary>

**False** — Not Given is for when the passage says nothing on the point at all.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. You're stuck on an unfamiliar word, and the clock is running. What should you check first?</strong></summary>

**Whether the current question actually depends on that word** — if not, skip it and keep moving.
