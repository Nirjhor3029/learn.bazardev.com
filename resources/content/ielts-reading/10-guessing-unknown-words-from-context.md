# Chapter 10 — Dealing With Unknown Words: Guessing Meaning From Context

> **Course:** IELTS Reading · **Chapter:** 10 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/10-guessing-unknown-words-from-context](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/10-guessing-unknown-words-from-context)

No dictionary is allowed in the exam. Every passage will contain words you don't know — that's expected, not a sign you're failing. The skill this chapter teaches is using everything **around** an unknown word to work out enough of its meaning to answer the question.

## 1. First: do you even need to know it?

Before hunting for clues, ask whether the unknown word actually blocks you from answering a question. Most unknown words don't — you can often skip them entirely and keep reading. Only stop and dig for meaning when the word sits right at the heart of a question you need to answer.

| Clue type | Signal words | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition / restatement** | "that is," "i.e.," "means," "refers to," "defined as" | A plain-language explanation follows directly |
| **Synonym** | No signal word — just a nearby word with a similar meaning | The writer is avoiding repetition; the nearby word is a stand-in |
| **Contrast / antonym** | "but," "however," "unlike," "in contrast," "whereas" | The unknown word likely means close to the **opposite** of what follows |
| **Example** | "such as," "for example," "including," "like" | The examples given narrow down the word's category and meaning |

## 3. A fifth clue: grammar

Even with zero context clues, the word's **part of speech** narrows things down. Is it after "the" (likely a noun)? Does it end in "-ly" (likely an adverb)? Does it follow "to" (likely a verb)? This alone often tells you enough to answer a question about function, even without the exact meaning.

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Stopping on every unknown word, panicking, and losing time. | Only dig for meaning when a question depends on that specific word. |
| Assuming an unknown word near "unlike" or "however" means the same as its neighbour. | Contrast signals point to the **opposite** meaning, not the same one. |
| Ignoring the word's grammar (noun/verb/adjective) entirely. | Part of speech alone can answer some questions, even without full meaning. |
| Trying to translate the unknown word into Bangla in your head first. | Work out its role in the English sentence directly — translating first wastes time. |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. "The device was cumbersome; the newer model, by contrast, is compact." What does "cumbersome" likely mean?</strong></summary>

**The opposite of "compact"** — something bulky or hard to carry, signalled by "by contrast."
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "Her findings were seminal — that is, foundational to all later research." What does "seminal" mean here?</strong></summary>

**Foundational** — the phrase "that is" directly restates the meaning.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. "Predators, such as wolves and hawks, limit prey populations." What does this tell you about "predators"?</strong></summary>

**It's a category** that includes wolves and hawks — animals that hunt other animals.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Should you stop and work out the meaning of every single unknown word you meet?</strong></summary>

**No** — only when a question specifically depends on that word; otherwise, skip it and keep reading.
</details>
