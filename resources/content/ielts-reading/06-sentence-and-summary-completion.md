# Chapter 6 — Sentence and Summary Completion

> **Course:** IELTS Reading · **Chapter:** 6 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/06-sentence-and-summary-completion](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/06-sentence-and-summary-completion)

This question type is unusual: even a perfect understanding of the passage doesn't guarantee marks, because the answer must also obey a strict **word limit** and fit the sentence **grammatically**. Getting the meaning right isn't enough on its own.

## 1. The word-limit rule — the single biggest mechanical trap

Exceeding the word limit scores **zero**, even with a perfectly correct meaning. Hyphenated words (like "well-known") usually count as **one** word — check the exact instructions each time.

## 2. Predicting the missing word's grammar

Before scanning, look at the words **around** the gap — they tell you what type of word must fill it.

## 3. Two summary-completion formats

| Format | How it works |
|---|---|
| **With a word bank** | Choose from a given list of words — spelling is never an issue, since you select the exact word provided. The list usually has **more words than gaps**, so some are distractors. |
| **Without a word bank** | You must find the exact word(s) **in the passage** yourself, respecting the word limit **and** spelling it correctly. |

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Writing "very significant increase" when the limit is two words. | Count your words and trim to fit the exact limit. |
| Writing the right word but misspelling it. | Copy the spelling **exactly** as it appears in the passage. |
| Filling a noun gap with a verb, because its meaning seemed close. | Check the grammar around the gap first — part of speech matters as much as meaning. |
| Expecting the summary to repeat the passage's exact wording. | The summary is almost always **paraphrased** — scan for synonyms, not identical phrases. |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. The instructions say "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS." You write a correct three-word phrase. What's your score for that question?</strong></summary>

**Zero** — exceeding the word limit scores nothing, regardless of meaning.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "The population grew ___ between 1990 and 2000." What part of speech fits the gap?</strong></summary>

**An adverb** (e.g., "rapidly") — it follows a verb, "grew."
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. If a summary completion task gives you a word bank, does spelling matter?</strong></summary>

**No** — you select the exact word from the list, so spelling is never at risk.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Without a word bank, what happens if you find the right word but misspell it?</strong></summary>

**You lose the mark** — spelling must match the passage exactly.
</details>
