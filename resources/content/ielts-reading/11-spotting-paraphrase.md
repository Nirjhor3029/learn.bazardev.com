| Paraphrase type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Synonym substitution** | "few" → "a small number of" |
| **Word-class change** | "succeed" (verb) → "be successful" (adjective); "decision" (noun) → "decided" (verb) |
| **Sentence restructuring** | Active → passive, or reordered clauses, saying the same thing differently |
| **Generalisation / specification** | "vehicles" → "cars" (specific), or "cars" → "vehicles" (general) |
| **Antonym + negation** | "rare" → "not common"; "increased" → "did not decrease" |

## 2. The keyword table method

Before scanning, list the question's key terms in one column, then brainstorm likely paraphrases for each in a second column. This is the technique experienced test-takers use to stay one step ahead of the passage's wording, instead of hunting for an exact word match that may never appear.

| Question keyword | Likely paraphrase to scan for |
|---|---|
| few | a small number of, hardly any, only a handful |
| researchers | scientists, experts, academics, investigators |
| believed | thought, held the view, were convinced |
| succeed | be successful, work, prove effective |

## 3. Why keyword-only scanning fails

A statement can share several exact words with the passage and still be **False** or **Not Given** — matching words is not the same as matching meaning. Always confirm that the *meaning* lines up, not just the vocabulary (this is exactly why Chapter 2's True/False/Not Given warns against "word-spotting").

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Scanning for the exact words from the question, expecting an identical match. | Scan for the *meaning*, using likely synonyms and paraphrases. |
| Assuming shared vocabulary means the statement is True. | Check that the full meaning agrees, not just a few overlapping words. |
| Treating a generalisation ("vehicles") and its specific form ("cars") as unrelated. | Recognise that general ↔ specific terms can paraphrase each other. |
| Missing a negated antonym ("not uncommon" = "common"). | Watch for double negatives and antonym + "not" combinations. |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. The question says "increased sharply." The passage says "did not decrease." Could these match?</strong></summary>

**Possibly** — "did not decrease" could paraphrase a rise, but check carefully: it only confirms "not falling," not necessarily "increased sharply."
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. A statement shares five exact words with the passage. Does that make it True?</strong></summary>

**Not necessarily** — matching words isn't matching meaning; the full sense of the statement still has to agree with the passage.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. The passage says "cars." The question says "vehicles." Is this a valid paraphrase?</strong></summary>

**Yes** — "vehicles" is a generalisation of "cars," a common paraphrase pattern.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. What's the point of building a keyword table before scanning?</strong></summary>

**To predict likely paraphrases** for each key term, so you can scan for meaning instead of hunting for an exact word match.
</details>
