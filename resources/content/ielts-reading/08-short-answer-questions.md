# Chapter 8 — Short-Answer Questions

> **Course:** IELTS Reading · **Chapter:** 8 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/08-short-answer-questions](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/08-short-answer-questions)

Short-answer questions look like sentence completion's cousin — same strict word limit — but they ask a direct question ("How many...?", "What does...?", "Where...?") instead of giving you a sentence with a gap. The good news: once you find the right spot in the passage, the answer is almost always **taken word-for-word**, not paraphrased.

## 1. The format

- You get a question (not a gapped sentence) and a word limit, e.g. "Answer using **NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER**."
- The answer is a word, number, or short phrase **copied directly from the passage**.
- Numbers count toward the limit: "25 October" is two words, not one.

- **Changeable keywords** (concepts, actions) are often paraphrased in the passage — don't scan for these literally.
- **Unchangeable keywords** (numbers, proper nouns, dates, specific quantities) rarely change — scan for these to locate the answer fast.
- Once located, the answer itself is usually **stated directly** — copy it exactly.

## 3. Why order helps (mostly)

Short-answer questions almost always follow the order the information appears in the passage, just like every other completion/matching type in this course. Use the question before and after as location anchors if you lose your place.

## 4. Grammar and copying rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Copy spelling exactly from the passage | Misspelling a correct word still loses the mark |
| Stay within the word limit, including numbers | "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" means two words max, a number counts as one |
| Make sure your answer fits the question grammatically | "Where...?" needs a place, not an action |
| Don't add articles (a/the) unless they're part of the exact phrase | Extra words can push you over the limit |

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Scanning for the changeable (paraphrasable) keyword and getting lost. | Scan for the unchangeable keyword (number, name, date) first. |
| Paraphrasing your own answer instead of copying the passage's exact words. | Copy the exact word/phrase — these answers are rarely paraphrased. |
| Forgetting a number counts as a word toward the limit. | Count numbers as words when checking the limit. |
| Assuming answers can appear in any order. | Expect the passage order to match the question order. |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. In the question "How many hours do adult bats sleep each day?", which part is the unchangeable keyword to scan for?</strong></summary>

**"How many hours"** (and the eventual number) — "adult bats" and "sleep" are changeable and may be paraphrased.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Is the final answer to a short-answer question usually paraphrased or copied directly?</strong></summary>

**Copied directly** from the passage, once you've located the right sentence.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. If the limit is "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" and the answer is "25 October," is that within the limit?</strong></summary>

**Yes** — "25" and "October" count as two words total.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Do short-answer questions usually follow the passage's order?</strong></summary>

**Yes**, almost always — use neighbouring questions as location anchors.
</details>
