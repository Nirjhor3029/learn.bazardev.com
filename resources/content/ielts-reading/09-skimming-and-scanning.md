# Chapter 9 — Skimming and Scanning: Reading Fast Without Missing What Matters

> **Course:** IELTS Reading · **Chapter:** 9 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/09-skimming-and-scanning](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/09-skimming-and-scanning)

Three passages, 40 questions, 60 minutes. There is no time to read every word carefully — you need two different reading speeds, used at the right moment.

## 1. Two speeds, two jobs

| | Skimming | Scanning |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Get the gist — what is each paragraph *about*? | Find one specific fact, name, number, or date |
| **Speed** | Fast, whole-passage pass | Fast, but targeted at one keyword |
| **What you read** | Titles, headings, topic sentences (often the first sentence of each paragraph) | Everything, but your eyes jump past anything that isn't the keyword |
| **When you use it** | Before answering any question — build a "paragraph map" | After you know exactly which word or fact you need |

## 2. Skim first: build a paragraph map

Spend 1-2 minutes skimming the whole passage before touching the questions. Read the title, then just the first (and sometimes last) sentence of each paragraph. You're not trying to understand everything — you're building a map of **which paragraph covers which topic**, so you know where to look later.

## 3. Then scan: hunt for one thing at a time

Once a question tells you what to look for, scan — don't read. Let your eyes jump down the paragraph looking **only** for the keyword type you need:

- A **number or date** → let your eyes catch digits, skip the words around them.
- A **name** → look for capital letters.
- A **specific term** → look for that exact word or its obvious synonym (see Chapter 11 on paraphrase).

This is exactly the "unchangeable keyword" strategy from Chapter 8, applied to any question type.

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Reading the passage top to bottom like a novel, word by word. | Skim first for structure, then scan for specific answers. |
| Skimming when you actually need an exact fact (and missing it). | Switch to scanning once you know exactly what you're looking for. |
| Scanning the whole passage for every question, every time. | Use your paragraph map to jump straight to the right paragraph first. |
| Re-reading the same paragraph in full for each new question. | Most questions in a passage follow its order — move forward, don't restart. |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. What is the goal of skimming, as opposed to scanning?</strong></summary>

**Getting the gist** — understanding what each paragraph is about, not finding one exact fact.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. When should you switch from skimming to scanning?</strong></summary>

**Once you know exactly what you're looking for** — a specific name, number, date, or term from a question.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. What's the fastest way to locate the right paragraph for a question?</strong></summary>

**Use your paragraph map** built from skimming, instead of re-scanning the whole passage.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Should you read every word carefully on your first pass through a passage?</strong></summary>

**No** — with 60 minutes for 40 questions across three passages, skim first and scan for specifics.
</details>
