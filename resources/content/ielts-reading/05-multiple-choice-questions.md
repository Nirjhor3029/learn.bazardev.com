# Chapter 5 — Multiple Choice Questions

> **Course:** IELTS Reading · **Chapter:** 5 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/05-multiple-choice-questions](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-reading/05-multiple-choice-questions)

Multiple choice looks the most familiar of all the question types, which is exactly why test-takers underestimate it. Unlike matching information (Chapter 4), **multiple choice questions follow the passage's order** — question 1's answer appears before question 2's.

## 1. Single-answer vs. multi-answer

| Format | What it asks | Key risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Single answer** | Choose 1 correct option from 4 (A-D) | Picking a close-but-wrong distractor |
| **Multi-answer** | Choose exactly 2 or 3 correct options from a longer list | Selecting too many or too few options — getting the exact count wrong usually scores zero for that question |

**Always check the instructions first** to see how many options you need to select.

The official IELTS.org guidance specifically warns about the "borrowed words" trap: an incorrect option often **includes a word straight from the passage**, which makes it feel right even though the overall claim is wrong.

If you can't point to the exact sentence in the passage that supports an option, don't select it — a correct answer should always be traceable to a specific sentence.

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Choosing an option because it reuses passage vocabulary. | Check whether the **overall claim** matches, not just a borrowed word. |
| Selecting 3 options when the instructions ask for exactly 2. | Always confirm the **required count** before finalising answers. |
| Re-reading the entire passage for every single option. | Since questions follow passage order, scan **forward** from where the last answer was found. |
| Picking the first "plausible-sounding" option without checking the others. | Read all options, then eliminate — don't stop at the first one that sounds right. |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. An option uses a word copied directly from the passage. Does that make it correct?</strong></summary>

**Not necessarily** — this is a classic distractor trap; check whether the overall claim actually matches.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. A multi-answer question asks for exactly 2 correct options, but you've selected 3. What should you do?</strong></summary>

**Recheck all 3** — at least one is likely wrong, since selecting the wrong count usually scores zero.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Do multiple choice questions follow the passage's order?</strong></summary>

**Yes** — unlike matching information (Chapter 4), multiple choice answers appear in the same order as the passage.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. What should you do before looking at the answer options?</strong></summary>

**Try to predict the answer yourself** from the question stem, then compare it to the options.
</details>
