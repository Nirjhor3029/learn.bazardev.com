# Chapter 1 — How IELTS Reading Is Scored, and the One Skill That Matters Most

> **Course:** IELTS Reading · **Chapter:** 1 of 12

Unlike Writing or Speaking, Reading has no examiner judging your style — every question has one objectively correct answer. That makes the test more mechanical than it looks, and this chapter explains the mechanics before any question-type chapter.

Your raw score (0-40) converts to the familiar 9-band scale. A blank answer scores zero — a wrong guess costs you nothing — so **every question should get an answer**, even a guess, before time runs out.

Almost no question repeats the passage's exact wording — it's nearly always **paraphrased**: different vocabulary, different sentence structure, same meaning. Every question type in this course — True/False/Not Given, matching headings, multiple choice, and the rest — is really testing the same underlying skill: **can you recognise when two different sentences mean the same thing?**

## 3. Skimming vs. scanning — different tools for different jobs

| Technique | What it's for | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Skimming** | Getting the gist and structure of a passage quickly, without reading every word | Before matching-headings questions, or to build a mental map of the passage |
| **Scanning** | Hunting for a specific keyword, name, date, or number | True/False/Not Given, short-answer, and most detail questions |

(Chapter 9 covers both techniques in depth.)

## 4. The golden rule: only what's written, nothing you already know

Reading questions test what the **passage states**, not your outside knowledge or common sense. A statement can be factually true in real life and still be marked **Not Given** if the passage never actually says it. This single rule trips up more test-takers than any vocabulary gap.

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. How many marks do you lose for a wrong guess?</strong></summary>

**Zero** — there's no negative marking, so always answer every question.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. The passage says "the city's population grew rapidly." The question says "there was a sudden increase in residents." What's happening here?</strong></summary>

**Paraphrase** — the same fact, expressed with different words and structure.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which technique is for hunting a specific date or name: skimming or scanning?</strong></summary>

**Scanning.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. A statement is true in real life, but the passage never mentions it. How should you answer a True/False/Not Given question about it?</strong></summary>

**Not Given** — the passage's content decides the answer, not outside knowledge.
</details>
