Every word-limit and grammar rule from Chapter 6 still applies here — the only difference is that the gaps sit inside a **visual structure**. That structure is actually a gift: it narrows down exactly where to look and what kind of word fits.

## 1. Tables: let the column headers do the work

A table's row and column headers tell you the **category** of the missing word before you even read the passage closely.

## 3. Diagrams: let the layout and existing labels do the work

For diagram labelling, the diagram itself usually already shows **some** labels and the overall layout (arrows, positions like "top," "left," "inlet"). Use those fixed points to work out roughly where in the passage the missing label's description must be — new technical terms introduced in the passage are almost always what diagram questions test.

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Treating a table gap like a plain-text summary gap, ignoring the column header. | Use the header to predict the **type** of word before scanning. |
| Picking a detail from the wrong step of a process for a flow-chart gap. | Track sequencing words (first, next, then, finally) to find the right step. |
| Guessing a diagram label from general knowledge instead of the passage. | The correct label is always **stated in the passage**, often as a term introduced for the first time. |
| Forgetting the word limit and grammar rules from Chapter 6 still apply. | Every rule from sentence/summary completion — word count, spelling, grammar fit — still applies here. |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. A table column is headed "Property," and a row is about steel. What should NOT go in the gap?</strong></summary>

**A material name** (like "wood" or "steel") — the column header signals a property word is needed instead.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. In a flow-chart, how do you find the right step to match a gap?</strong></summary>

**Track sequencing words** in the passage (first, next, then, finally) to locate the matching stage.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Where does the correct answer for a diagram label always come from?</strong></summary>

**The passage** — often a technical term introduced there for the first time, not outside/general knowledge.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Do the word-limit and spelling rules from Chapter 6 still apply to table/flow-chart/diagram completion?</strong></summary>

**Yes** — this question type is a visual variant of the same completion rules.
</details>
