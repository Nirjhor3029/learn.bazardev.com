# Chapter 2 — Part 1: Introduction and Interview

> **Course:** IELTS Speaking · **Chapter:** 2 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-speaking/02-part-1-introduction-and-interview](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-speaking/02-part-1-introduction-and-interview)

Part 1 opens the test: the examiner checks your identity, then asks familiar, everyday questions — home, work or study, hobbies, daily routine. It's designed to put you at ease, not to trip you up. The most common score-limiting mistake here isn't grammar — it's answering in one word and stopping.

Typical topics: where you live, your work or studies, your hometown, hobbies, food, how you spend weekends. None of it is designed to be difficult — it's designed to sound like a real conversation.

## 2. The one-word-answer trap

| Question | One-word answer (too short) | Fuller answer (what examiners want) |
|---|---|---|
| "Do you work or are you a student?" | "Student." | "I'm a student — I'm studying business administration at a university in Dhaka, in my final year." |
| "Do you like your hometown?" | "Yes." | "Yes, quite a lot, actually. It's a smaller city, so it's much quieter than Dhaka, and I like that I can get everywhere on foot." |

A one-word answer gives the examiner almost nothing to score — not enough Fluency, not enough Lexical Resource, not enough grammar to judge. **Extend every answer with a reason, an example, or a comparison**, even when the question sounds like a yes/no question.

## 3. A simple extension formula

For almost any Part 1 question, answer in three moves:

1. **Answer directly** — don't dodge the question.
2. **Add why, or a detail** — "because…", "mainly…", "what I mean is…".
3. **Add a small example or comparison** — a specific memory, a contrast with something else.

This alone turns a 3-second answer into a natural 15-20 second one, without memorising anything — because the content comes from your own real life, not a script.

## 4. What Part 1 is *not* testing

- It's **not** testing whether your life is interesting — a plain answer delivered fluently and clearly scores better than an exciting answer delivered haltingly.
- It's **not** the place for a cue-card-style prepared speech — long monologues to a simple personal question can sound rehearsed and odd.
- The examiner **will not** argue or disagree with you here — Part 1 isn't a debate, that comes later in Part 3.

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. What's the main purpose of Part 1?</strong></summary>

To **put you at ease** with familiar, everyday personal questions — not to challenge you.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Why is a one-word answer like "Yes" risky in Part 1?</strong></summary>

It gives the examiner **too little language to score** across fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. What are the three moves in the extension formula from this chapter?</strong></summary>

**Answer directly → add a reason or detail → add a small example or comparison.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Should you give a long, prepared-sounding speech in response to a simple Part 1 question?</strong></summary>

**No** — that style belongs to Part 2's cue card, not Part 1's short, natural exchange.
</details>
