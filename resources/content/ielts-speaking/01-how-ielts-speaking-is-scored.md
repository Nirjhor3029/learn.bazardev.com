# Chapter 1 — How IELTS Speaking Is Scored, and the Four Criteria Examiners Actually Use

> **Course:** IELTS Speaking · **Chapter:** 1 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-speaking/01-how-ielts-speaking-is-scored](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-speaking/01-how-ielts-speaking-is-scored)

Speaking is the only IELTS section with no multiple choice, no "correct" sentence to find, and no second read — just you and a certified examiner, talking, for about 11 to 14 minutes. That can feel like the least predictable section. It isn't: the examiner is scoring four fixed criteria, the same four every time, and this course is built around getting good at all four, not just "sounding confident."

The test is identical in format, timing, and difficulty whether you're taking IELTS Academic or General Training — Speaking doesn't change between the two.

## 2. The three parts

| Part | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Part 1** | ~4-5 min | The examiner asks personal, familiar questions (home, work/study, hobbies) to warm you up |
| **Part 2** | ~3-4 min | You get a **cue card** with a topic, 1 minute to prepare notes, then talk for up to 2 minutes, followed by 1-2 short questions on the same topic |
| **Part 3** | ~4-5 min | A discussion with the examiner, built on Part 2's theme, but more abstract and opinion-based |

Chapters 2 through 5 cover each part in detail.

1. **Fluency and Coherence** — can you keep talking without long pauses or restarting, and does one idea connect logically to the next? (Chapter 6.)
2. **Lexical Resource** — how wide is your vocabulary, and can you paraphrase instead of repeating the same words? (Chapter 7.)
3. **Grammatical Range and Accuracy** — do you use a mix of simple *and* complex sentence structures, mostly correctly? (Chapter 8.)
4. **Pronunciation** — can the examiner understand you easily, with natural stress and intonation? (Chapter 9.)

## 4. What Speaking does *not* score

A few common misconceptions, worth clearing up before you prepare:

- **Your accent is not marked.** Examiners are trained to understand a wide range of accents — a Bangladeshi or South Asian accent is not a penalty by itself. Pronunciation is scored on clarity, not on sounding British or American.
- **Confidence alone isn't a criterion.** A nervous but grammatically accurate, clear answer scores on its actual language, not on how relaxed you looked.
- **Talking more isn't automatically better.** A long answer that wanders off-topic can lose marks for coherence — length only helps when it's relevant.

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. How many parts does the Speaking test have, and how long does the whole test take?</strong></summary>

**Three parts**, taking about **11 to 14 minutes** in total.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. How many criteria make up your Speaking band score, and what share does each one carry?</strong></summary>

**Four criteria**, each worth an equal **25%**: Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, and Pronunciation.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Is a Bangladeshi or South Asian accent penalised in the Pronunciation score?</strong></summary>

**No** — examiners score clarity, not accent. A strong accent that's still easy to understand is not marked down for being an accent.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Does Part 2 use multiple choice or written questions?</strong></summary>

**Neither** — Part 2 gives you a **cue card** with a topic and 1 minute to prepare, then you speak for up to 2 minutes.
</details>
