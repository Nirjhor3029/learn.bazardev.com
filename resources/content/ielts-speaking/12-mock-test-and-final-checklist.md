**How to set it up with just your phone:**

1. **Part 1** — write 6-8 personal questions in advance (home, work/study, hobbies, daily routine). Set a timer for 4-5 minutes, read each question aloud yourself (or have a study partner ask), and record your answers.
2. **Part 2** — write a cue card topic (reuse one of the five categories from Chapter 10), give yourself exactly 1 minute to prepare with keywords, then record yourself speaking for up to 2 minutes.
3. **Part 3** — write 4-5 follow-up questions that generalise your Part 2 topic (e.g. if Part 2 was about a skill you learned, Part 3 might ask "why do some skills matter more than others in today's job market?"). Record your answers, 4-5 minutes total.

## 2. Reviewing your own recording

Listen back once straight through without judging, then a second time checking against the four criteria from Chapters 6-9:

| Criterion | What to listen for |
|---|---|
| Fluency & Coherence | Long pauses? Logical linking between ideas? |
| Lexical Resource | Same words repeated? Did you paraphrase when stuck? |
| Grammatical Range | Any complex sentences at all, or all short/simple? |
| Pronunciation | Word-by-word delivery, or natural phrase chunks? |

Pick just **one** thing to improve for your next practice round — trying to fix everything at once is harder than fixing one habit repeatedly.

## 3. The combined final checklist

**Before the test:**
1. Have you prepared **keyword ideas** across the five topic categories (Chapter 10), not memorised scripts?
2. Do you know your own common grammar slips to watch for (Chapter 8)?

**During Part 1:**
3. Are you extending every answer with a reason or example, not stopping at one word (Chapter 2)?

**During Part 2:**
4. Are you using the full 1-minute prep for keywords, not a script (Chapter 3)?
5. If the topic feels unfamiliar, are you ready to borrow and bend, or use the 5 W's (Chapter 4)?

**During Part 3:**
6. Are you shifting to general, society-level language, not just personal stories (Chapter 5)?
7. Are you stating a clear position and giving a reason, even when unsure (Chapter 5)?

**Throughout all three parts:**
8. Are you avoiding long silent pauses and excessive "umm" (Chapter 6)?
9. Are you paraphrasing instead of freezing when a word is forgotten (Chapter 7)?
10. Are you mixing in at least some complex sentence structures (Chapter 8)?
11. Are you stressing content words and chunking phrases naturally (Chapter 9)?

## 4. Quick practice

<summary><strong>Q1. How long should a self-run Part 2 mock be, including prep time?</strong></summary>

**1 minute of preparation, plus up to 2 minutes of speaking** — the same timing as the real test.
