## 3. Two extra traps worth naming directly

- **Parroting the question back.** Repeating the examiner's exact words at the start of your answer ("Do I like my hometown? Well, do I like my hometown...") doesn't demonstrate paraphrase and can read as a stalling tactic if overused. A brief natural lead-in ("That's an interesting question...") is fine; repeating the question verbatim every time is not.
- **Going off-topic for length.** As Chapter 1 noted, more words aren't automatically better — an answer that drifts away from the actual question loses Coherence marks even if it's fluent and grammatically solid.

## 4. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. What's the single biggest risk of a one-word Part 1 answer?</strong></summary>

It gives the examiner **too little language** to score fairly across all four criteria.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Is it fine to use a written script during Part 2's 1-minute prep time?</strong></summary>

**No** — keywords only; reading a script aloud sounds unnatural and hurts Fluency.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. What theme covers mistakes like short, simple sentences and repeated vocabulary?</strong></summary>

**Narrow range** — the theme behind Chapters 7 and 8's mistakes.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Why can repeating the examiner's question word-for-word at the start of your answer be a problem?</strong></summary>

It can read as a **stalling tactic** and doesn't demonstrate real paraphrasing ability if overused.
</details>
