# Chapter 7 — Lexical Resource

> **Course:** IELTS Speaking · **Chapter:** 7 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-speaking/07-lexical-resource](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-speaking/07-lexical-resource)

Lexical Resource is your vocabulary range — but not in the way most candidates assume. It isn't about dropping the fanciest word you know into every sentence. It's about **flexibility**: using varied, precise, natural vocabulary, and paraphrasing smoothly when you don't know — or forget — a specific word.

A high scorer doesn't necessarily use rare words — they use the **right** everyday word consistently, with enough variety to avoid sounding repetitive.

## 2. Paraphrasing: what to do when a word won't come

This is the single most useful Lexical Resource skill, and it's a direct extension of what's scored well in Reading and Writing: **describe the idea instead of stopping**.

| Forgotten word | Paraphrase instead |
|---|---|
| "umbrella" | "the thing you use to keep dry when it rains" |
| "generous" | "someone who likes to share what they have with others" |
| "commute" | "the journey you make every day to get to work" |

A fluent paraphrase scores *better* than a long silent pause while you search for the exact word — the examiner sees that you can communicate the idea regardless.

## 3. Collocation: words that travel in pairs

Natural-sounding English relies heavily on words that are typically used together, not just individually correct words:

- ✅ "make a decision" / ❌ "do a decision"
- ✅ "have a preference" / ❌ "make a preference"
- ✅ "pay attention" / ❌ "give attention"
- ✅ "a close friend" / ❌ "a near friend"

You don't need a huge list memorised — reading and listening to natural English (Chapters in the Reading and Listening courses help here too) builds this intuition over time.

## 4. Avoiding repetition, without forcing "big words"

If a question keeps using the word "important," repeating "important" three times in your answer is fine grammatically, but sounds flat. Simple fixes:

- Swap in a near-synonym: "significant," "crucial," "matters a lot"
- Rephrase the structure instead of just swapping one word: "This is something I really value" instead of "This is important to me"

Avoid forcing in advanced vocabulary you're not fully confident with just to sound impressive — a misused rare word hurts more than a correctly used simple one.

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. What does Lexical Resource actually reward — rare words, or something else?</strong></summary>

**Flexibility and range** — using varied, precise, natural vocabulary, not necessarily rare words.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. If you forget a specific word mid-answer, what should you do?</strong></summary>

**Paraphrase** — describe the idea in other words, rather than pausing silently or stopping.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which collocation is correct: "make a decision" or "do a decision"?</strong></summary>

**"Make a decision"** — "do" doesn't naturally pair with "decision" in English.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Is it a good idea to force in advanced vocabulary you're not confident using correctly?</strong></summary>

**No** — a misused rare word hurts your score more than a correctly used simple one.
</details>
