## 1. Worked example: Problem-solution essay

> **Prompt:** *In many cities, traffic congestion has become a serious problem. What are the causes of this problem, and what solutions can be suggested?*

**[Paraphrase + outline]** Traffic congestion has become an increasingly serious issue in urban areas worldwide. This essay will examine the main causes of this problem before suggesting several possible solutions.

**[Body 1 — causes]** One of the primary causes of traffic congestion is the rapid growth in private car ownership. As incomes rise, more people choose to buy cars rather than rely on public transport, which they often see as slower or less comfortable. As a result, city roads, originally designed for far fewer vehicles, become overwhelmed during peak hours. In many rapidly developing cities, for instance, the number of registered vehicles has doubled within a decade, far outpacing any expansion of road infrastructure.

**[Body 2 — solutions]** A combination of improved public transport and targeted restrictions on car use could significantly ease this problem. If buses and trains are made faster, cheaper, and more frequent, commuters have a genuine alternative to driving. Measures such as congestion charges in city centres further discourage unnecessary car trips. London's congestion charge, introduced in 2003, noticeably reduced traffic volumes within the charging zone within its first few years.

**[Conclusion]** In conclusion, while rising car ownership is the main driver of urban traffic congestion, a combination of better public transport and targeted charges offers a realistic way to reduce it.

## 2. Worked example: Advantage-disadvantage essay

> **Prompt:** *Nowadays, more and more people choose to work from home. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this trend?*

**[Paraphrase + outline]** An increasing number of employees now work remotely rather than commuting to a traditional office. This essay will discuss both the benefits and the drawbacks of this growing trend.

**[Body 1 — advantages]** Working from home offers considerable advantages, particularly in terms of flexibility and reduced commuting time. Employees can structure their day around personal responsibilities, and the hours previously spent travelling can be redirected toward work or rest. This often leads to higher job satisfaction and a better work-life balance. Surveys of remote workers frequently report improved morale compared with office-based colleagues facing long daily commutes.

**[Body 2 — disadvantages]** On the other hand, remote work can create significant challenges, especially around collaboration and social isolation. Without regular face-to-face contact, misunderstandings between colleagues become more frequent, and spontaneous teamwork is harder to replicate online. Many remote workers report feeling disconnected from their organisation over time. Several technology companies that fully embraced remote work during the pandemic later asked staff to return to the office partly for this reason.

**[Conclusion, balanced]** Overall, although remote work offers valuable flexibility and improved work-life balance, its drawbacks in collaboration and social connection mean that a hybrid approach may suit most organisations best.

## 3. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| A problem-solution essay proposes a vague fix: "people should be more careful." | Propose specific, realistic solutions, like "introduce congestion charges" or "expand bus frequency." |
| An advantage-disadvantage essay spends 90% of the word count on advantages. | Give both sides roughly **equal** space and development. |
| The solutions paragraph doesn't address the causes named earlier. | Make sure each solution clearly responds to a cause raised in the causes paragraph. |
| The advantage-disadvantage conclusion suddenly argues strongly for one side. | Keep the conclusion **balanced**, unless the prompt explicitly asks for an opinion. |

## 4. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. In the problem-solution essay, which sentence names the main cause of traffic congestion?</strong></summary>

**"One of the primary causes of traffic congestion is the rapid growth in private car ownership."**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Why must each proposed solution connect back to a cause raised earlier?</strong></summary>

**Because a solution that doesn't address a named cause feels disconnected and weakens Task Response.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. In an advantage-disadvantage essay, how should the two body paragraphs be balanced?</strong></summary>

**Roughly equally** — neither side should dominate the word count.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Should the conclusion of an advantage-disadvantage essay argue strongly for one side, unless asked?</strong></summary>

**No** — it should stay balanced unless the prompt explicitly asks for an opinion.
</details>
