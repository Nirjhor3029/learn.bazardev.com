# Chapter 9 — Task 2: Developing Ideas with Explanation and Examples

> **Course:** IELTS Writing · **Chapter:** 9 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/09-task2-developing-ideas-with-examples](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/09-task2-developing-ideas-with-examples)

The official Task Response criterion rewards ideas that are **"extended and supported."** The most common way essays fall short isn't bad grammar — it's a topic sentence followed by a single vague sentence, with no real reasoning or example in between. This chapter gives a repeatable technique for extending any idea.

**Worked example (topic: public transport funding):**

*Claim:* Investing in public transport reduces traffic congestion. *Because:* when buses and trains are reliable and affordable, more commuters choose them over driving. *As a result:* fewer cars are on the road during peak hours. *For example:* in Seoul, a major investment in bus and subway services in the early 2000s led to a measurable drop in private car use.

## 3. Where examples come from (and what to avoid)

Examiners and IELTS prep guides agree: strong examples usually come from **general knowledge, personal experience, or a well-known real-world case** — not invented statistics.

- ✅ *"For instance, in many Scandinavian countries, generous parental leave has been linked to a more balanced division of household labour."*
- ✅ *"In my own city, the introduction of a free trial week for public buses noticeably increased ridership."*
- ❌ *"Studies show that exactly 73.4% of people agree with this."* — an oddly specific, unverifiable number invented on the spot sounds artificial and can undermine credibility rather than strengthen it.

If you don't have a real statistic, use a **hedged general claim** instead: *"research suggests," "it is widely believed," "evidence increasingly points to."*

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| "This is a serious problem. For example, it is bad." | Chain the idea: claim → because → as a result → a concrete example. |
| "For example, many people think this is true." *(not actually an example)* | A real example names a specific case, place, or scenario — not another opinion. |
| Inventing a precise, unverifiable statistic. | Use a real-world case or a hedged general claim instead. |
| One sentence per idea, then straight to the next paragraph. | Spend 3-4 sentences developing each main idea before moving on. |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Which is a real example: "many people think this is true" or "in Seoul, bus investment reduced private car use"?</strong></summary>

**"In Seoul, bus investment reduced private car use"** — it names a specific place and outcome; the other is just another vague opinion.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Why is inventing a precise statistic like "73.4% of people agree" risky?</strong></summary>

**It's unverifiable and can sound artificial**, which can undermine credibility rather than support the argument.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. What are the four steps in the chain-of-reasoning technique?</strong></summary>

**Claim → because → as a result → for example.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. If you don't have a real statistic, what should you use instead?</strong></summary>

**A hedged general claim** — e.g., "research suggests" or "it is widely believed."
</details>
