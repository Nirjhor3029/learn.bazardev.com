# Chapter 10 — Task 2: Opinion and Discussion Essays, Worked Examples

> **Course:** IELTS Writing · **Chapter:** 10 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/10-task2-opinion-and-discussion-essays](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/10-task2-opinion-and-discussion-essays)

Chapters 6-9 covered question types, structure, thesis statements, and developing ideas separately. This chapter puts all of it together into two complete, annotated essays — one Opinion, one Discussion.

## 1. Worked example: Opinion essay

> **Prompt:** *Some people think that the government should invest more money in public transport rather than building new roads. To what extent do you agree or disagree?*

**[Paraphrase + thesis]** Many argue that government funds would be better spent improving public transport systems instead of expanding road networks. I completely agree with this view, mainly because public transport reduces congestion more effectively and causes less environmental damage than new roads.

**[Body 1 — topic sentence → because → as a result → example]** Investing in public transport directly reduces traffic congestion. When buses and trains are frequent, reliable, and affordable, commuters are far more likely to leave their cars at home. As a result, fewer vehicles compete for the same road space during peak hours. For instance, cities that have expanded their metro networks, such as Seoul, have seen private car usage fall substantially over the following decade.

**[Body 2 — topic sentence → because → as a result → example]** Expanding road networks, by contrast, tends to worsen rather than solve long-term traffic problems. New roads often encourage more people to drive, a phenomenon economists call "induced demand." As a result, emissions and congestion typically increase rather than decrease over time. In my own country, a newly built highway outside the capital became just as congested as the roads it was meant to relieve, while also increasing local air pollution.

**[Conclusion — restate, no new ideas]** In conclusion, I firmly believe that public transport investment is the more effective and sustainable choice for governments, since it eases congestion directly and avoids the environmental costs that new roads tend to create.

## 2. Worked example: Discussion essay

> **Prompt:** *Some people think that students should be given one long holiday each year, while others believe it is better to have several short breaks throughout the year. Discuss both views and give your opinion.*

**[Paraphrase + outline]** There are differing opinions on whether students benefit more from one long holiday or from several shorter breaks spread across the year. This essay will examine both perspectives before explaining why I lean toward the latter.

**[Body 1 — view A]** Supporters of one long holiday argue that it allows for deeper rest and more ambitious personal projects. A continuous block of several weeks gives students enough time to travel, learn a new skill, or fully disconnect from academic pressure. As a result, many return with stronger motivation when term resumes. For example, students who spend a long summer break on an internship often come back with clearer career goals.

**[Body 2 — view B]** On the other hand, advocates of frequent short breaks point to the risk of burnout during long, unbroken terms. Several shorter holidays spread throughout the year give students regular opportunities to recover before exhaustion sets in. This can help maintain more consistent academic performance across the whole year. In countries with a mid-term break every six to eight weeks, teachers often report that students stay more engaged than those facing one long, unbroken term.

**[Opinion, in the conclusion]** Overall, although both arrangements have genuine merit, I believe several shorter breaks better protect students' wellbeing and sustain academic performance throughout the year.

## 4. A self-check before you submit

- Did the introduction **paraphrase** the question, not copy it?
- Does the thesis/outline statement match what the body paragraphs actually deliver?
- Does every body paragraph follow **topic sentence → because → as a result → example**?
- For a discussion essay — are **both views presented fairly**, with the opinion clear by the end?
- Does the conclusion **restate** the position without adding anything new?

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. In the opinion essay above, which sentence is the thesis?</strong></summary>

**"I completely agree with this view, mainly because public transport reduces congestion more effectively and causes less environmental damage than new roads."**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. In the discussion essay, where does the writer's own opinion appear?</strong></summary>

**In the conclusion** — after both views have been presented fairly in the body paragraphs.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which four-step pattern does each body paragraph in the opinion essay follow?</strong></summary>

**Topic sentence → because → as a result → example** (the chain-of-reasoning technique from Chapter 9).
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Why doesn't the discussion essay's introduction state a firm opinion right away?</strong></summary>

**Because a discussion essay must present both views fairly first** — stating an opinion too early would unbalance the essay.
</details>
