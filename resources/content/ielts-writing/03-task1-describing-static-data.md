# Chapter 3 — Task 1: Describing Static Data (Pie Charts, Tables, and Mixed Charts)

> **Course:** IELTS Writing · **Chapter:** 3 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/03-task1-describing-static-data](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/03-task1-describing-static-data)

Pie charts and tables usually show a snapshot — no change over time, just proportions and comparisons. The four-paragraph structure from Chapter 2 still applies; only the vocabulary changes, from trend words to **proportion and comparison** words.

| Proportion | Phrases |
|---|---|
| ~50% | just over/under half, approximately half |
| ~33% | roughly a third, approximately one in three |
| ~25% | a quarter, one in four |
| ~75% | three-quarters, the vast majority |
| ~10% or less | a small minority, a tiny fraction |
| the largest single group | accounts for the largest proportion/share |

**Key verbs:** *account for, make up, represent, constitute* — e.g., *"Housing **accounts for** 40% of total spending."*

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| X times as + adjective + as | *Country B's output was **three times as high as** Country A's.* |
| significantly/considerably higher/lower than | *Spending on health was **significantly higher than** spending on defence.* |
| roughly/approximately the same as | *The two figures were **roughly the same**.* |
| whereas / while (contrast) | *France spent heavily on education, **whereas** Germany prioritised healthcare.* |
| the highest/lowest proportion of | *Japan had **the highest proportion of** elderly residents.* |

## 3. Combining proportion and change (two pie charts, before/after)

When two pie charts show the same categories at two different times, combine proportion language with trend language from Chapter 2:

*"The proportion of spending on housing **rose from** 20% **to** 35% between 2000 and 2020, while the share spent on food **fell** over the same period."*

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Housing is 40% of spending. | Housing **accounts for** 40% of spending. *(use a reporting verb, not just "is")* |
| The two figures was the same. | The two figures **were** roughly the same. *(plural subject needs "were" — see IELTS Grammar Ch. 7)* |
| Food is three time more than housing. | Food spending was **three times** as high **as** housing spending. *("times" needs an -s, and the "as...as" structure)* |
| I think housing costs too much. | *(no opinion — Task 1 reports data only, exactly as in Chapter 2)* |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. "Housing ___ 40% of total spending." (is / accounts for)</strong></summary>

**accounts for** — a proper reporting verb, not the bare verb "is".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Rewrite using "roughly a third": "33% of respondents chose option A."</strong></summary>

**Roughly a third of respondents chose option A.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Fix: "Country B's output was three time as high as Country A's."</strong></summary>

**Country B's output was three times as high as Country A's.** — "times" needs the "-s".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Which word signals contrast between two categories: "whereas" or "moreover"?</strong></summary>

**Whereas** — it introduces a contrast; "moreover" adds similar information.
</details>
