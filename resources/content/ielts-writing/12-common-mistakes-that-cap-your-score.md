## 2. One trap per chapter — the master list

| # | Topic | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| [2](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/02-task1-describing-trends) | Trends | "Sales increased by 500 units." (when 500 is the final total) | "Sales **increased to** 500 units." *("to" marks the end point, "by" marks the size of the change)* |
| [3](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/03-task1-describing-static-data) | Static data | "Housing is 40% of spending." | "Housing **accounts for** 40% of spending." *(use a proper reporting verb)* |
| [4](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/04-task1-processes-and-maps) | Processes/maps | "Someone collect the raw material first." | "First, the raw material **is collected**." *(passive voice, no named doer)* |
| [5](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/05-task1-writing-a-strong-overview) | Overview | *(no overview at all)* | **Always** include 1-2 overview sentences — skipping it is the single most common, most costly Task 1 mistake. |
| [6](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/06-task2-the-five-question-types) | Question types | Treating "discuss both views" like a pure opinion essay. | Present **both** views fairly, **then** give your opinion. |
| [7](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/07-task2-essay-structure) | Essay structure | The whole essay is one long paragraph. | Use four clear paragraphs: intro, body 1, body 2, conclusion. |
| [8](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/08-task2-thesis-and-topic-sentences) | Thesis | Thesis promises two reasons; the essay only develops one. | Every point promised in the thesis **must** get its own body paragraph. |
| [9](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/09-task2-developing-ideas-with-examples) | Developing ideas | An idea is asserted but never explained. | Chain it: **claim → because → as a result → example.** |
| [10](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/10-task2-opinion-and-discussion-essays) | Opinion/discussion | A discussion essay states an opinion in the introduction, too early. | Present both views first; give the opinion in the **conclusion**. |
| [11](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-writing/11-task2-problem-solution-and-advantage-disadvantage-essays) | Problem-solution/adv-disadv | A proposed solution is vague: "people should be more careful." | Propose **specific, realistic** measures — e.g., "stricter fines," "mandatory training." |

## 3. The combined pre-submission checklist

**Task 1:**
1. Did you include an **overview** (1-2 sentences, no specific numbers) right after the paraphrase?
2. Did you avoid any **personal opinion**?
3. Did you reach the **150-word minimum**?

**Task 2:**
4. Did you correctly identify the **question type** before planning?
5. Did you answer **every part** of the question (especially two-part and discussion prompts)?
6. Does your thesis/outline statement **match** what the body paragraphs actually deliver?
7. Does each body paragraph follow **topic sentence → because → as a result → example**?
8. Did you reach the **250-word minimum**?

**Both tasks:**
9. Four clear paragraphs, correctly structured?
10. No invented, unverifiable statistics?
11. A final read-through for grammar and spelling?

## 4. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Which single Task 1 mistake is both the most common and most costly?</strong></summary>

**Skipping the overview entirely.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. A discussion essay states the writer's opinion in the introduction. What's wrong?</strong></summary>

**It's too early** — both views should be presented first; the opinion belongs in the conclusion.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which criterion does "topic sentence → because → as a result → example" most directly support?</strong></summary>

**Task Achievement/Response** (by developing ideas fully) — and indirectly Coherence and Cohesion.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Which course in this track builds Grammatical Range and Accuracy most directly?</strong></summary>

**IELTS Grammar** — comparatives for Task 1, passive voice for processes, linking words for cohesion, and more.
</details>
