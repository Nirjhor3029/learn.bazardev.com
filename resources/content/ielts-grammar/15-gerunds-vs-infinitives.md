# Chapter 15 — Gerunds vs. Infinitives

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 15 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/15-gerunds-vs-infinitives](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/15-gerunds-vs-infinitives)

A **gerund** is a verb + **-ing** used like a noun (*swimming*). An **infinitive** is **to** + the base verb (*to swim*). When one verb is followed by a second verb, the first verb decides which form the second one must take — and there's no shortcut rule, only patterns to learn.

These patterns are mostly arbitrary — there's no logical rule for why "enjoy" takes "-ing" but "want" takes "to". They have to be **memorised**, ideally by reading and noticing them, not by reasoning them out.

"Try" works similarly: *"Try restarting the computer"* (an experiment — see if it fixes the problem) vs. *"Try to finish on time"* (make an effort, even if difficult).

## 3. Verbs that can take either, with no meaning change

A few verbs genuinely don't mind which form follows: **like, love, hate, start, begin, continue** — *"I like swimming"* and *"I like to swim"* mean almost the same thing. (Note: "like" is different from "would like", which always takes an infinitive: *I would like to swim*, never "I would like swimming".)

## 4. Make, let, and help: the bare infinitive

**Make** and **let** are followed by an object + the **bare infinitive** (no "to"): *She made him apologise.* / *Let me help.* ("Help" itself can go either way: *help me do it* or *help me to do it*.)

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I enjoy to read books. | I enjoy **reading** books. *("enjoy" always takes a gerund)* |
| She decided going to the party. | She decided **to go** to the party. *("decide" always takes an infinitive)* |
| I'm interested to learn Spanish. | I'm interested **in learning** Spanish. *(after a preposition, always a gerund)* |
| She made him to apologise. | She made him **apologise**. *("make" + object + bare infinitive, no "to")* |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fix: "I enjoy to read books."</strong></summary>

**I enjoy reading books.** — "enjoy" always takes a gerund.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "She decided ___ to the party." (go / going / to go)</strong></summary>

**to go** — "decide" always takes an infinitive.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which has the different meaning: "Remember to call her" or "Remember calling her"?</strong></summary>

**"Remember calling her"** recalls a memory of having already called; **"Remember to call her"** is a reminder not to forget to do it.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Fix: "I'm interested to learn Spanish."</strong></summary>

**I'm interested in learning Spanish.** — after a preposition ("in"), the verb must be a gerund.
</details>
