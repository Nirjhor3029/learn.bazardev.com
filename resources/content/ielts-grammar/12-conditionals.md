Conditionals are "if" sentences. The whole system sounds complicated because there are five names to remember, but really it's just answering two questions: **is this real or imaginary?** and **are we talking about now/future, or the past?**

## 2. All five, in one table

| Type | Structure | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero | If + present simple, present simple | general truths, facts, rules | *If you heat ice, it melts.* |
| First | If + present simple, will + base verb | real, possible future | *If it rains, I'll stay home.* |
| Second | If + past simple, would + base verb | hypothetical / unlikely, now or future | *If I had more time, I would travel more.* |
| Third | If + past perfect, would have + past participle | an imagined, different past (often regret) | *If I had studied, I would have passed.* |
| Mixed | If + past perfect, would + base verb *(or the reverse)* | past condition, present result (or vice versa) | *If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.* |

## 4. Watch out for these specific traps

- **Never use "would" inside the if-clause itself:** *"If I would have more time..."* is wrong — it's *"If I **had** more time..."* ("would" only goes in the result).
- **"If I were you"**, not "If I was you" — in formal/second-conditional "if" clauses, "were" is used for all subjects (I, he, she, it), though "was" is common informally.
- **Second vs. third — present/future vs. past:** *"If I had money, I would buy it"* (second, about now) vs. *"If I had had money, I would have bought it"* (third, about a finished past situation).
- **"Unless" = "if ... not":** *"Unless you hurry, you'll miss the bus"* = *"If you don't hurry, you'll miss the bus."*

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| If I would have time, I would help you. | If I **had** time, I would help you. *("would" never goes in the if-clause)* |
| If I was you, I would apologise. | If I **were** you, I would apologise. *(formal second conditional)* |
| If she studied harder, she would have passed the exam last year. | If she **had studied** harder, she **would have passed** the exam last year. *(both clauses need past perfect/would have, for a past event)* |
| If it will rain, I will stay home. | **If it rains**, I will stay home. *(no "will" in the if-clause of a first conditional)* |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fix: "If I would have more time, I would travel."</strong></summary>

**If I had more time, I would travel.** — "would" never goes inside the if-clause.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Which type: "If water reaches 100°C, it boils."</strong></summary>

**Zero conditional** — a general scientific truth, always true.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Fix: "If she studied harder, she would have passed the exam last year."</strong></summary>

**If she had studied harder, she would have passed the exam last year.** — a finished past event needs the third conditional throughout.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Which conditional: "If I had taken that job, I would be living in London now."</strong></summary>

**Mixed conditional** — a past unreal condition ("had taken") with a present-day result ("would be living... now").
</details>
