# Chapter 11 — Passive Voice

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 11 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/11-passive-voice](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/11-passive-voice)

In an **active** sentence, the subject does the action. In a **passive** sentence, the subject *receives* the action — useful when the doer is unknown, obvious, or simply not the point. IELTS loves the passive for two specific jobs: describing **processes/diagrams** (Writing Task 1) and sounding **objective and academic** (Writing Task 2).

The **"by + doer"** phrase at the end is **optional** — in fact, leaving it out is normal when the doer is obvious, unknown, or unimportant: *"The bridge was built in 1998."* (we don't need to say who built it).

## 2. The passive across tenses (always: a form of "be" + past participle)

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | makes | is/are made |
| Past simple | made | was/were made |
| Present continuous | is making | is being made |
| Present perfect | has made | has been made |
| Past perfect | had made | had been made |
| Modal | must make | must be made |

Only the **"be"** part changes with tense — the past participle (*made*) never changes.

## 4. Passive for an objective, academic tone (Task 2)

In formal writing, the passive hides the writer's own opinion behind general knowledge or research, which sounds more objective:

- *It is widely believed that social media affects teenagers' mental health.* *(not "I believe...")*
- *Several studies have shown that...* → *It has been shown by several studies that...*
- *Mistakes were made in handling the crisis.* *(deliberately avoids blaming anyone specific)*

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| The report was wrote by the committee. | The report **was written** by the committee. *(past participle, not past simple)* |
| The cake is make by my mother. | The cake **is made** by my mother. *(the "be" verb must agree and match the tense)* |
| English is spoke in many countries. | English **is spoken** in many countries. |
| The window broke by the storm. | The window **was broken** by the storm. *("broke" is active — the window didn't break itself here)* |

**One real limit:** not every verb can be passive. Only **transitive** verbs (verbs with an object) can — *"He arrived"* has no object, so there's no passive form *"was arrived"*.

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Turn into passive: "The company produces 500 cars a day."</strong></summary>

**500 cars are produced a day (by the company).** — object becomes subject, verb becomes "are produced".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Fix: "The letter was wrote yesterday."</strong></summary>

**The letter was written yesterday.** — the passive always uses the past participle, not the past simple form.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which sounds more objective for an essay: "I think pollution is a serious problem" or "Pollution is widely considered a serious problem"?</strong></summary>

**"Pollution is widely considered a serious problem"** — the passive removes the personal "I think" for a more academic tone.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Fix: "The bridge build in 2005."</strong></summary>

**The bridge was built in 2005.** — passive needs "was/were" + past participle, not the bare past participle alone.
</details>
