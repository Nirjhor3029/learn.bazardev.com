# Chapter 16 — Linking Words and Cohesion

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 16 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/16-linking-words-and-cohesion](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/16-linking-words-and-cohesion)

IELTS Writing is scored partly on **"coherence and cohesion"** — a Band 7+ answer needs a *range* of linking words used *correctly*, not the same "and"/"but" repeated, and not every sentence stuffed with "moreover" either. The two words that trip up almost everyone are **although** and **despite** — they mean the same thing but need completely different grammar after them.

"However" is an adverb, not a conjunction like "but" — it can't join two full sentences with just a comma. Start a new sentence (or use a semicolon) instead.

## 3. The main categories you need

| Function | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Addition | also, in addition, furthermore, moreover | *The city is polluted. **Furthermore**, traffic is a constant problem.* |
| Contrast (clause) | but, although, even though, while, whereas | *The plan was risky, **but** it worked.* |
| Contrast (noun/gerund) | despite, in spite of | *They succeeded **despite** the odds.* |
| Cause (clause) | because, since, as | *We stayed inside **because** it was raining.* |
| Cause (noun) | because of, due to | *The delay was **because of** the weather.* |
| Result | so, as a result, therefore, consequently | *It rained heavily; **as a result**, the match was cancelled.* |

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Despite it was raining, we went out. | **Despite the rain**, we went out. *(despite + noun, not a clause)* |
| I went to his house, however he wasn't home. | I went to his house. **However**, he wasn't home. *(new sentence, not a comma)* |
| Because of it was raining, we stayed in. | **Because it was raining**, we stayed in. *("because" + clause; "because of" + noun)* |
| The exam was hard but, I passed it. | The exam was hard, **but I passed it**. *(no comma right after "but")* |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fix: "Despite it was raining, we went out."</strong></summary>

**Despite the rain, we went out.** (or "Despite it raining") — "despite" needs a noun or gerund, never a full clause.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Fix: "I went to his house, however he wasn't home."</strong></summary>

**I went to his house. However, he wasn't home.** — "however" starts a new sentence; it can't join two clauses with just a comma.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. "The delay was ___ the weather." (because / because of)</strong></summary>

**because of** — followed by a noun phrase, not a full clause.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Which shows result: "so" or "although"?</strong></summary>

**"so"** — it introduces a result/consequence; "although" introduces a contrast.
</details>
