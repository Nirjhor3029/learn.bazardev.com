# Chapter 3 — Nouns: countable vs uncountable, and quantifiers

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 3 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/03-nouns-countable-uncountable-and-quantifiers](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/03-nouns-countable-uncountable-and-quantifiers)

Chapter 2 told you that plural and uncountable nouns usually take **no article** in general statements. This chapter answers the question that creates: **how do you even know if a noun is countable or not?** It also gives you the exact words (*many, much, a few, a number of…*) you'll lean on constantly in IELTS Writing Task 1, where almost every sentence is really just describing a quantity of something.

## 1. The basic split

A **countable** noun can be counted one by one — it has a singular and a plural form (*one book, two books*). An **uncountable** noun names something you think of as a whole, a mass, or a substance — you can't say "two waters" or "three musics."

## 2. The trap: nouns that *feel* countable but aren't

This is where most mistakes happen — some nouns that feel like individual, countable things are grammatically uncountable in English:

| Uncountable (no plural, no "a/an") | Not this |
|---|---|
| information | ~~an information, informations~~ |
| advice | ~~an advice, advices~~ |
| news | ~~a news, newses~~ |
| furniture | ~~a furniture, furnitures~~ |
| luggage / baggage | ~~a luggage, luggages~~ |
| equipment | ~~an equipment, equipments~~ |
| traffic | ~~a traffic~~ |
| work (as in "employment") | ~~a work, works~~ (meaning "job") |

If you need to count one of these, add a countable "unit" word in front: **a piece of information**, **a bit of advice**, **an item of furniture**, **a piece of equipment**.

**One nuance worth learning now:** *few/little* (no "a") mean "almost none — not enough," while *a few/a little* mean "some — a small positive amount."

- *I have **few** friends here.* → sounds a bit sad/negative — almost no friends.
- *I have **a few** friends here.* → neutral/positive — some friends, enough to be fine.

## 4. For IELTS Writing Task 1: describing quantities in data

Task 1 charts are full of quantities, so these formal phrases are worth learning now instead of overusing "a lot of" in every sentence:

| Phrase | Rough meaning |
|---|---|
| a significant/substantial number of | many (emphasised) |
| the majority of | most |
| a small proportion of | a small percentage of |
| a considerable amount of | much (emphasised, usually uncountable) |
| a minority of | fewer than half |

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I need an advice. | I need **some advice.** / I need **a piece of advice.** |
| There is too many traffic today. | There is too **much** traffic today. |
| She has much friends. | She has **many** friends. |
| I have few money, so I'm careful. | I have **little** money, so I'm careful. *(negative meaning — not enough)* |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. "many" or "much"? "There isn't ___ time left."</strong></summary>

**much** — "time" is uncountable.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Fix it: "He gave me three advices about the interview."</strong></summary>

**He gave me some advice about the interview.** (or "three pieces of advice") — "advice" has no plural.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. "few" or "a few"? Which one sounds more positive: "I made ___ friends at the new job" (meaning it went fine)?</strong></summary>

**a few** friends — positive, "some, and that's okay."
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Rewrite for IELTS Task 1: "A lot of people chose option A."</strong></summary>

A more formal option: **"The majority of people chose option A."** or **"A significant number of people chose option A."**
</details>
