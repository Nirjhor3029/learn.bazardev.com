# Chapter 13 — Relative Clauses

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 13 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/13-relative-clauses](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/13-relative-clauses)

A relative clause adds information about a noun using **who, which, that, whose, where, or when**. The single hardest thing about them isn't the pronoun — it's knowing when a **comma** changes the entire meaning of the sentence.

## 1. Which pronoun for which noun

| Pronoun | Refers to | Example |
|---|---|---|
| who | people (subject or object) | *the woman **who** called you* |
| which | things, animals | *the car **which** broke down* |
| that | people or things — **defining clauses only** | *the book **that** I read* |
| whose | possession (people or things) | *the man **whose** car was stolen* |
| where | places | *the city **where** I was born* |
| when | times | *the year **when** I graduated* |

**Rule that follows from this:** a non-defining clause (with commas) can **never** use "that" — only who/which/whose. And you can **never** drop the pronoun from a non-defining clause.

## 3. Dropping the pronoun (only sometimes possible)

You can omit who/which/that when it's the **object** of the relative clause (not the subject):

- *The book (that) I read was great.* — "I read **the book**" → "book" is the object, so "that" is optional.
- *The woman who called you is my aunt.* — "**who** called you" — "who" is the **subject** of "called", so it **cannot** be dropped: *"The woman called you is my aunt"* is wrong.

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| The woman which called you is my aunt. | The woman **who** called you is my aunt. *("which" is for things, not people)* |
| My brother, that lives in Dhaka, is a doctor. | My brother, **who** lives in Dhaka, is a doctor. *("that" is never used in a non-defining, comma'd clause)* |
| The man who's car was stolen called the police. | The man **whose** car was stolen called the police. *("who's" = "who is"; "whose" shows possession)* |
| The woman called you is my aunt. | The woman **who** called you is my aunt. *(can't drop "who" here — it's the subject of "called")* |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fix: "The woman which called you is my aunt."</strong></summary>

**The woman who called you is my aunt.** — "which" is for things; "who" is for people.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Does this sentence imply one brother or more than one? "My brother who lives in Dhaka is a doctor."</strong></summary>

**More than one** — no commas means it's a defining clause, identifying which specific brother.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Can "that" be dropped here: "The book that I read was great"?</strong></summary>

**Yes — "The book I read was great."** "That" is the object of "read", so it's optional.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Fix: "The man who's car was stolen called the police."</strong></summary>

**The man whose car was stolen called the police.** — "whose" shows possession; "who's" means "who is".
</details>
