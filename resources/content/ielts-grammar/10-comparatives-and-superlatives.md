# Chapter 10 — Comparatives and Superlatives

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 10 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/10-comparatives-and-superlatives](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/10-comparatives-and-superlatives)

A **comparative** compares two things (*taller than*). A **superlative** picks out the extreme of three or more (*the tallest*). This is the single most useful grammar tool for IELTS Writing Task 1, where you're constantly comparing numbers in a chart or graph.

**Irregular forms you must memorise:** good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst; far → farther/further → the farthest/furthest; little → less → the least; many/much → more → the most.

**A few two-syllable adjectives (ending in -y, -er, -ow, -le) allow either form:** *happier/more happy*, *simpler/more simple*.

## 2. "Than" with comparatives, "the" with superlatives

- Comparative + **than**: *China's output was higher **than** Japan's.*
- Superlative usually takes **the**: *China had **the** highest output of all five countries.*

Phrases that come up constantly in Task 1 reports:

- *The figure for X was **significantly higher than** the figure for Y.*
- *X had **by far the largest** share of the total.*
- *The gap between X and Y **grew wider and wider** over the period.* *(repeated comparative = continuous change)*
- *X and Y were **roughly the same**, while Z was **considerably lower**.*

## 4. Modifying comparatives: making them stronger or weaker

| Modifier | Strength | Example |
|---|---|---|
| much / far / a lot | much stronger | *China's output was **much higher than** Japan's.* |
| a little / slightly / marginally | only a small difference | *The figure was **slightly lower** in 2020.* |
| by + amount | exact difference | *The population grew **by 2 million** more than the previous decade.* |

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| This is more bigger than that one. | This is **bigger than** that one. *(never combine "more" with "-er")* |
| She is the most tallest in the class. | She is **the tallest** in the class. *(never combine "most" with "-est")* |
| Than 2015, sales were higher. | **In 2015**, sales were higher **than** in 2016. *("than" compares two things, it doesn't mean "in")* |
| It is more good than the other one. | It is **better than** the other one. *("good" is irregular — not "more good")* |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fix: "This year's profit was more higher than last year's."</strong></summary>

**This year's profit was higher than last year's.** — never combine "more" with "-er".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "Of the five countries, Brazil had ___ population." (the most large / the largest)</strong></summary>

**the largest** — "large" is a short adjective, so it takes "-est", not "most".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Fix: "She is more good at Maths than her brother."</strong></summary>

**She is better at Maths than her brother.** — "good" is irregular: good → better → best.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Which shows continuous change over time: "colder" or "colder and colder"?</strong></summary>

**"colder and colder"** — a repeated comparative shows a trend that keeps progressing, useful for describing a line graph.
</details>
