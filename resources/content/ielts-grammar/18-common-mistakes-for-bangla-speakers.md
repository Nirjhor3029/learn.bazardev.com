You've now covered every grammar topic this roadmap set out to teach. This final chapter does two things: it pulls together the **single worst mistake from each of the previous 17 chapters** into one table you can scan before an exam, and it covers a handful of **extra Bangla-to-English translation traps** that don't belong to any one earlier topic — mostly from carrying Bangla sentence habits straight into English.

## 1. One trap per chapter — the master list

| # | Topic | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| [1](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/01-parts-of-speech-and-sentence-basics) | Sentence basics | Yesterday I to market went. | Yesterday **I went to** the market. *(English is Subject-Verb-Object; Bangla is Subject-Object-Verb)* |
| [2](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/02-articles-a-an-the) | Articles | I am engineer. | I am **an** engineer. *(Bangla has no articles — English almost always needs one)* |
| [3](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/03-nouns-countable-uncountable-and-quantifiers) | Countable/uncountable | I have many informations. | I have **a lot of information**. *("information" is uncountable — no plural "s")* |
| [4](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/04-present-tenses) | Present tenses | I am knowing the answer. | I **know** the answer. *("know" is a state verb — never continuous)* |
| [5](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/05-past-tenses) | Past tenses | I was going there yesterday. | I **went** there yesterday. *(a finished single past action needs past simple)* |
| [6](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/06-future-forms) | Future forms | I will go there, I already decided. | I'**m going to** go there — I already decided. *(a pre-made decision uses "going to", not "will")* |
| [7](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/07-subject-verb-agreement) | Subject-verb agreement | The list of items are long. | The list of items **is** long. *(the real subject is "list", not "items")* |
| [8](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/08-questions-and-word-order) | Questions and word order | Could you tell me where is the station? | Could you tell me where **the station is**? *(embedded questions keep statement order)* |
| [9](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/09-modal-verbs) | Modal verbs | You mustn't come if you're busy. *(meant as optional)* | You **don't have to** come if you're busy. *("mustn't" = forbidden, not optional)* |
| [10](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/10-comparatives-and-superlatives) | Comparatives/superlatives | This is more bigger than that. | This is **bigger than** that. *(never combine "more" with "-er")* |
| [11](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/11-passive-voice) | Passive voice | English is spoke in many countries. | English **is spoken** in many countries. *(past participle, not past simple)* |
| [12](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/12-conditionals) | Conditionals | If I would have time, I would help. | If I **had** time, I would help. *("would" never goes inside the if-clause)* |
| [13](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/13-relative-clauses) | Relative clauses | The woman which called you is my aunt. | The woman **who** called you is my aunt. *("which" is for things, not people)* |
| [14](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/14-reported-speech) | Reported speech | She said she is tired now. | She said she **was tired then**. *(backshift the tense AND the time word)* |
| [15](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/15-gerunds-vs-infinitives) | Gerunds vs. infinitives | I enjoy to read books. | I enjoy **reading** books. *("enjoy" always takes a gerund)* |
| [16](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/16-linking-words-and-cohesion) | Linking words | Despite it was raining, we went out. | **Despite the rain**, we went out. *("despite" + noun/gerund, never a clause)* |
| [17](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/17-complex-sentences-and-punctuation) | Complex sentences | It was raining, we stayed inside. | It was raining, **so** we stayed inside. *(a comma alone can't join two full sentences)* |

More preposition pairs worth memorising directly (since there's no shortcut rule):

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I am angry on you. | I am angry **with** you. |
| depend of | depend **on** |
| good in English | good **at** English |
| arrived to the station | arrived **at** the station |
| listen music | listen **to** music *(a few verbs, like "listen" and "look", need "to"/"at" before their object)* |

## 3. A final mental checklist before you submit

1. **Articles** — does every singular countable noun have a/an/the (or a deliberate reason for none)?
2. **Subject-verb agreement** — did a long subject with words in between trick you?
3. **Tense** — does it match the actual time (finished past vs. still relevant vs. future)?
4. **Word order** — any embedded ("Could you tell me...") questions wrongly inverted?
5. **Linking words** — "although" with a clause, "despite" with a noun — not mixed up?
6. **Sentence boundaries** — any comma splices or fragments hiding in a long sentence?
