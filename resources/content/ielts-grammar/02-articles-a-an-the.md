# Chapter 2 — Articles: a, an, the — and when to use none

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 2 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/02-articles-a-an-the](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/02-articles-a-an-the)

Bangla has no word that works like "a," "an," or "the." So when a Bangla speaker writes in English, the brain's default is simply **to leave the article out** — because there's no instinct telling it one is missing. This single gap is one of the most common ways IELTS Writing and Speaking lose accuracy marks, even from otherwise strong candidates. The good news: there are only 4 possible choices, and a short, learnable set of rules decides between them every time.

The decision really comes down to two questions, asked in order:

1. **Is it countable, and are you talking about just one?** If not (it's plural, or it's uncountable like *water*, *advice*, *music*), skip straight to question 2.
2. **Is it a specific thing the reader/listener can identify** (already mentioned, pointed at, or the only one that exists)? → use **the**. **Not specific** → use **a/an** (if singular countable) or **no article** (if plural or uncountable).

## 2. "a" or "an"? It's about sound, not spelling

This is the rule most learners get only half-right: **the choice depends on the *sound* the next word starts with — not the letter.**

If you can say the phrase out loud and hear whether it starts with a vowel sound (ah, eh, ih, oh, uh) or a consonant sound, you'll pick correctly every time — even for words you've never seen before.

## 3. "Zero article" — when you use nothing at all

This is the case Bangla speakers already get right by instinct (since it matches "no article" in Bangla too) — but it's worth knowing the rule so you can tell it apart from a mistake:

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| Plural noun, general statement | *Teachers deserve more respect.* |
| Uncountable noun, general statement | *Music helps people relax.* |
| Most proper nouns (names of people, countries, cities) | *Bangladesh is in South Asia.* |
| Languages, nationalities as adjectives | *She speaks Bangla and English.* |
| Meals, sports, academic subjects, in general | *I play football. He studies chemistry.* |

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong (missing article) | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I am going to market. | I am going to **the** market. *(a specific, known market)* |
| She is good student. | She is **a** good student. |
| I want to buy book. | I want to buy **a** book. |
| Sun rises in east. | **The** sun rises in **the** east. *(only one sun, only one east)* |

Notice the pattern: every "wrong" sentence above has a singular countable noun with **no article at all** — that's the exact gap this chapter is fixing.

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fill the gap: "I need ___ umbrella — it's raining."</strong></summary>

**an** umbrella — "umbrella" starts with a vowel *sound* (uh), and it's the first time it's mentioned (not specific yet).
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Fill the gap: "___ Pacific Ocean is the largest ocean in the world."</strong></summary>

**The** Pacific Ocean — it's a specific, unique, one-of-a-kind thing.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Fill the gap: "___ honesty is the best policy."</strong></summary>

**(no article)** — "honesty" is uncountable and this is a general statement about it, not one specific instance.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. "a" or "an"? "___ hour", "___ university", "___ honest man"</strong></summary>

**an hour** (silent h, vowel sound) · **a university** (starts with a "yoo" consonant sound) · **an honest man** (silent h, vowel sound).
</details>
