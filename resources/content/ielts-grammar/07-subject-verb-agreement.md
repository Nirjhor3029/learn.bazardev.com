# Chapter 7 — Subject-Verb Agreement

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 7 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/07-subject-verb-agreement](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/07-subject-verb-agreement)

This rule sounds simple — "singular subject, singular verb" — and it is, until the sentence gets longer. Almost every subject-verb mistake in IELTS Writing happens in a *longer* sentence, where extra words sit between the subject and its verb and quietly confuse which one the verb should match.

## 2. The #1 trap: words in between don't count

The verb must agree with the **real subject**, never with a noun that just happens to sit closer to it.

The same trap, the other way round: *"The reasons for his absence **are** unclear."* — the subject is "reasons" (plural), not "absence."

## 3. Tricky cases worth memorising

| Case | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Collective nouns (team, family, government) | usually singular — treated as one unit | *The team **is** winning.* |
| Indefinite pronouns (everyone, everybody, nobody, someone, each) | always singular | *Everyone **has** arrived.* |
| each / every + noun | always singular | *Every student **has** a book.* |
| either...or / neither...nor | the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | *Neither the teacher nor the students **were** ready.* |
| subjects joined by "and" | usually plural | *Tom and Jerry **are** friends.* |
| "a number of" + plural noun | plural verb | *A number of students **have** passed.* |
| "the number of" + plural noun | singular verb (the subject is "the number") | *The number of students **has** increased.* |
| there is / there are | matches the noun that follows | *There **are** three books on the table.* |

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| The list of items are on the table. | The list of items **is** on the table. *("list" is the subject, not "items")* |
| Everyone have finished the test. | Everyone **has** finished the test. *("everyone" is always singular)* |
| The number of accidents have increased. | The number of accidents **has** increased. *("the number of" takes a singular verb)* |
| Neither of the options are correct. | Neither of the options **is** correct. *("neither" on its own is singular)* |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. "The reasons for his absence ___ unclear." (is / are)</strong></summary>

**are** — the subject is "reasons" (plural), not "absence."
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "Neither the manager nor the staff ___ aware of the change." (was / were)</strong></summary>

**were** — the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "the staff" (plural).
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. "A number of candidates ___ already applied." (has / have)</strong></summary>

**have** — "a number of + plural noun" takes a plural verb.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. "The number of candidates ___ risen sharply this year." (has / have)</strong></summary>

**has** — "the number of" is itself the (singular) subject here.
</details>
