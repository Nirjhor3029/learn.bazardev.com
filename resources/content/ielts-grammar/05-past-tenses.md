The hardest part isn't any one of these four tenses on its own — it's **telling two past events apart when you need to say which one happened first.** That's exactly what past perfect and past perfect continuous exist for, and it's a very common gap in IELTS Speaking Part 2 (telling a story about the past).

## 2. Form and meaning, one by one

| Tense | Form | Core meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Past Simple | verb-ed (or irregular form) | one finished action/state at a specific past time |
| Past Continuous | was/were + verb-**ing** | an action in progress at a past moment, often interrupted |
| Past Perfect | had + past participle | an earlier past event, finished before a later past event |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been + verb-**ing** | a duration that was happening up until a later past event |

## 3. The key skill: which one happened first?

Whenever a story has **two past events**, English marks which one came first with the tense, not with extra words:

> *"When I **arrived**, she **had left**."* → she left **first**, then I arrived.
> *"When I **arrived**, she **left**."* → she left **at the moment I arrived** (both roughly together) — a different meaning, from the same two verbs, changed only by tense.

This is exactly the tool for IELTS Speaking Part 2 (a 2-minute story about the past): using past perfect for the "earlier" event makes your story's timeline completely unambiguous — which is part of what "accuracy" is scoring.

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| When I arrived, she already left. | When I arrived, she **had already left**. |
| I was living in Dhaka in 2015. (one-time fact) | I **lived** in Dhaka in 2015. *(past continuous wrongly used for a simple fact)* |
| She had been study for 3 hours before the exam. | She **had been studying** for 3 hours before the exam. |
| I didn't went to the party. | I **didn't go** to the party. *(after "didn't," always the base form)* |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. "While I ___ (cook) dinner, the power went out."</strong></summary>

**was cooking** — past continuous (ongoing action) interrupted by a past simple event ("went out").
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "By the time the ambulance arrived, the man ___ (already / die)."</strong></summary>

**had already died** — this happened before the ambulance arrived, so it needs past perfect.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Fix it: "I was knowing the answer, but I didn't say it."</strong></summary>

**I knew the answer, but I didn't say it.** — "know" is a stative verb; it doesn't take the continuous form.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. "She ___ (study) English for two years before she moved to Canada."</strong></summary>

**had been studying** — past perfect continuous: the duration was happening up until the later past event ("moved").
</details>
