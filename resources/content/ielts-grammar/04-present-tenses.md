# Chapter 4 — Present Tenses: Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 4 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/04-present-tenses](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/04-present-tenses)

English has 4 "present" tenses, and each one describes a different **shape** of time — not just "now." Bangla speakers often default to one simple present form for everything, because Bangla doesn't carve up "now" into four separate shapes the way English does. Seeing all four on the same timeline, side by side, is the fastest way to feel the difference.

## 2. Form and meaning, one by one

| Tense | Form | Core meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | base verb (+s for he/she/it) | habits, facts, routines, timetables |
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + verb-**ing** | happening right now, or a temporary situation |
| Present Perfect | have/has + past participle | a past action with no stated time, connected to now (experience, result, unfinished time period) |
| Present Perfect Continuous | have/has been + verb-**ing** | an action that started in the past and is still continuing, with emphasis on duration |

## 3. The one that trips up Bangla speakers most: Present Perfect

Bangla doesn't have a direct equivalent of the present perfect, so the biggest risk is accidentally mixing it with the past simple. The rule that fixes almost every mistake:

> 🎯 **If there is a specific time word (yesterday, last year, in 2020, two days ago), you must use the past simple — never the present perfect.** Present perfect is for when the *exact time doesn't matter*; only the connection to now does.

- ❌ *I have visited Cox's Bazar last year.* (specific time word "last year" → can't use present perfect)
- ✅ *I visited Cox's Bazar last year.* (past simple)
- ✅ *I have visited Cox's Bazar.* (no time given — it's about the experience, not when)

**"For" vs "since"** (used constantly with the perfect tenses):
- **for** + a duration — *for two years, for a week, for a long time*
- **since** + a starting point — *since 2020, since Monday, since I was a child*

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I am understanding the lesson now. | I **understand** the lesson now. *(stative verbs like understand, know, want, believe don't take -ing)* |
| She lives here since 2019. | She **has lived** here since 2019. |
| I have seen him yesterday. | I **saw** him yesterday. *(specific time word → past simple)* |
| I am study English every day. | I **study** English every day. *(habit → present simple, not continuous)* |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. "I ___ (write) an email right now — can you wait a minute?"</strong></summary>

**am writing** — happening at this exact moment → present continuous.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "Water ___ (boil) at 100°C." — which tense, and why?</strong></summary>

**boils** — present simple. It's a general fact, always true, not a one-time event.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Fix it: "I have finished my homework yesterday."</strong></summary>

**I finished my homework yesterday.** — "yesterday" is a specific time word, so it must be past simple.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. "She ___ (work) here for six months." — which tense?</strong></summary>

**has been working** — present perfect continuous, because it emphasises the ongoing duration ("for six months") of something that is still true.
</details>
