By now you know enough grammar to build long, complex sentences — the risk is building them *incorrectly*. This chapter covers the three most common structural errors: **fragments**, **comma splices**, and **run-ons**, plus the one comma rule that fixes most of them.

## 1. Independent vs. dependent clauses

An **independent clause** has a subject and a verb and can stand alone as a complete sentence. A **dependent clause** also has a subject and verb, but starts with a word (*because, although, when, if, since, while...*) that makes it unable to stand alone.

## 3. Comma splices and run-ons (joining two complete sentences wrongly)

| Error | What it looks like | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Comma splice | *It was raining, we stayed inside.* | Two independent clauses joined with **only a comma** |
| Run-on (fused) | *It was raining we stayed inside.* | Two independent clauses joined with **no punctuation at all** |

**Four correct fixes:**

1. Split into two sentences: *It was raining. We stayed inside.*
2. Add a coordinating conjunction after the comma: *It was raining, **so** we stayed inside.*
3. Use a semicolon: *It was raining; we stayed inside.*
4. Turn one clause into a dependent clause: ***Because** it was raining, we stayed inside.*

## 4. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Because it was raining. We stayed inside. | **Because it was raining, we stayed inside.** *(join the fragment to its main clause)* |
| It was raining, we stayed inside. | It was raining, **so** we stayed inside. *(comma splice — add a conjunction, or use a period/semicolon)* |
| We stayed inside, because it was raining. | We stayed inside **because** it was raining. *(no comma when the independent clause comes first, in most cases)* |
| Studying every day, this is important for IELTS. | **Studying every day is important for IELTS.** *(the first part needs to connect grammatically, not float as its own fragment)* |

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fix: "Because it was raining. We stayed inside."</strong></summary>

**Because it was raining, we stayed inside.** — join the dependent clause (fragment) to the independent clause.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Fix (comma splice): "It was raining, we stayed inside."</strong></summary>

**It was raining, so we stayed inside.** (or a period, or a semicolon) — a comma alone can't join two independent clauses.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Does this need a comma: "We stayed inside because it was raining"?</strong></summary>

**No** — when the independent clause comes first, a comma usually isn't needed before the dependent clause.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Is this a fragment or a complete sentence: "Although the exam was difficult"?</strong></summary>

**A fragment** — "although" makes it a dependent clause; it needs a main clause attached, e.g. "Although the exam was difficult, I passed."
</details>
