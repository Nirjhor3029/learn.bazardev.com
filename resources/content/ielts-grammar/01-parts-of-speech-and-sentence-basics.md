# Chapter 1 — Parts of Speech and Sentence Basics

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 1 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/01-parts-of-speech-and-sentence-basics](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/01-parts-of-speech-and-sentence-basics)

Every other chapter in this course — tenses, articles, conditionals, passive voice — is really just a more detailed rule about the two things you'll learn here: **what job each word does in a sentence**, and **what order those jobs go in**. Get comfortable with these two ideas first, and every chapter after this one will be much easier to follow.

This chapter is also the only one you need before you can start noticing your own mistakes — which is the fastest way to raise your IELTS grammar score, in both Writing and Speaking.

## 1. Why this matters for your IELTS score

IELTS examiners score your grammar on two things, in both Speaking and Writing:

- **Range** — do you use a *variety* of structures (not just simple sentences, over and over)?
- **Accuracy** — are the structures you use actually correct?

You cannot build range or accuracy without first knowing the names of the pieces you're working with. If a teacher (or this course) says "you're missing an article here" or "this needs to be in the passive voice," that sentence is only useful to you if you know what an *article* or the *passive voice* actually is. That's the whole point of this chapter.

## 2. The 8 parts of speech

Every word in English does one of these 8 jobs. Some words can do more than one job depending on the sentence (e.g. "run" can be a verb or a noun) — don't worry about that yet, just learn to recognise the 8 jobs first.

A quick reference, in one sentence each:

- **Noun** — the name of a person, place, thing, or idea: *teacher, Dhaka, laptop, freedom*.
- **Pronoun** — replaces a noun so you don't repeat it: *I, you, he, she, it, they, this*.
- **Verb** — the action or state of the sentence: *run, eat, is, become, believe*.
- **Adjective** — describes a noun: *happy, tall, cheap, difficult*.
- **Adverb** — describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb, often answering "how/when/where/how much": *quickly, very, yesterday, outside*.
- **Preposition** — links a noun to the rest of the sentence, usually about place, time, or direction: *in, on, at, to, from, with*.
- **Conjunction** — joins two words, phrases, or ideas: *and, but, or, because, although*.
- **Article / determiner** — points to a noun and tells you something about it (which one, how many): *a, an, the, this, some*.

## 3. Basic sentence anatomy: Subject + Verb + Object

Here is the single biggest structural difference between Bangla and English — and the one habit that fixes the most grammar mistakes at once.

**Bangla sentences put the verb last.** **English sentences put the verb right after the subject**, in the middle of the sentence. Look at the same sentence in both languages:

Notice the green "verb" box is in the **middle** for English but at the **end** for Bangla. This is why a direct word-for-word translation from Bangla so often comes out wrong in English — the words are right, but the order is still Bangla order.

> 🎯 **The one habit to build:** before you finish any English sentence, check that your verb comes right after your subject — not at the end. *"I rice eat"* is Bangla word order wearing English words. *"I eat rice"* is correct.

Most English sentences follow one of these two patterns:

| Pattern | Example | When |
|---|---|---|
| Subject + Verb (S-V) | *The baby cried.* | the verb doesn't act on anything |
| Subject + Verb + Object (S-V-O) | *I eat rice.* / *She passed the exam.* | the verb acts on something |

## 4. Quick practice

Try to answer before opening each one.

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Label each word's part of speech: "The confident student answered quickly."</strong></summary>

*The* = article · *confident* = adjective (describes "student") · *student* = noun · *answered* = verb · *quickly* = adverb (describes "answered").
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Fix the word order: "I English every day study."</strong></summary>

**I study English every day.** — the verb ("study") must come right after the subject ("I"), not at the end.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Is this sentence S-V or S-V-O? "She smiled."</strong></summary>

**S-V.** "Smiled" doesn't act on anything, so there's no object.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Is this sentence S-V or S-V-O? "He finished the report."</strong></summary>

**S-V-O.** "Finished" acts on "the report," which is the object.
</details>

## 5. The mistake to watch for from here on

From this chapter onward, whenever you write or say an English sentence, do one silent check: **find your verb, and make sure it's in the middle — right after the subject — not at the end.** This single check catches a huge share of the word-order mistakes Bangla speakers make in IELTS Writing and Speaking. We'll come back to this, and other common Bangla-to-English mistakes, in detail in [Chapter 18](/ielts-grammar/18-common-mistakes-for-bangla-speakers) once you've learned the rest of the pieces.
