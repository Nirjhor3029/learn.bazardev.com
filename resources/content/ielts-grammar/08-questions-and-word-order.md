# Chapter 8 — Questions and Word Order

> **Course:** IELTS Grammar · **Chapter:** 8 of 18
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/08-questions-and-word-order](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-grammar/08-questions-and-word-order)

Direct questions and indirect (embedded) questions follow **opposite** word-order rules. Mixing them up is one of the most common Speaking and Writing mistakes Bangla speakers make — and it's completely fixable once you see the pattern.

## 1. Direct questions: the auxiliary flips to the front

To turn a statement into a yes/no question, the subject and the auxiliary verb **swap places**.

If the statement has **no auxiliary** (present simple or past simple with a main verb), add **do / does / did** to the front instead of inventing an inversion:

- *She works here.* → **Does** she work here? *(not "Works she here?")*
- *They arrived late.* → **Did** they arrive late?

For wh-questions, the question word goes first, then the same swap happens: **Where is she going?**, **What did you buy?**, **Why does he leave early?**

## 2. Subject questions don't invert — this trips people up

When **who / what / which** is itself the subject of the sentence (not the object), there's no auxiliary and no inversion — the word order stays exactly like a statement.

| Asking about the... | Example | Word order |
|---|---|---|
| **object** (needs do/does/did + subject) | *What* **did you buy**? | Wh-word + aux + subject + verb |
| **subject** (no aux needed at all) | *Who* **called** you? | Wh-word (= subject) + verb, just like a statement |

Compare: *"Who did call you?"* is wrong — "who" is already the subject here, so it behaves like *"Rahim called you"*, not like a question that needs an auxiliary.

Another pair: *"What happened?"* (subject question, no "did") vs *"What did you do?"* (object question, needs "did").

## 3. The big IELTS trap: indirect (embedded) questions

When a question is **embedded** inside a longer, more polite sentence — *"Could you tell me...", "Do you know...", "I wonder...", "I'd like to know..."* — the embedded part goes back to **statement word order**. No inversion, no extra do/does/did.

More examples of the same pattern:

- *Could you tell me* **what time the museum opens**? *(not "what time does the museum open")*
- *I wonder* **why she left early**. *(not "why did she leave")*
- *Do you know* **where he lives**? *(not "where does he live")*

## 4. Yes/no indirect questions need "if" or "whether"

A direct yes/no question has no question word to embed, so the indirect version adds **if** or **whether** instead:

- Direct: *Did she arrive on time?*
- Indirect: *I wonder* **if/whether** *she arrived on time.*

- Direct: *Is the exam difficult?*
- Indirect: *Could you tell me* **if/whether** *the exam is difficult?*

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Could you tell me where is the nearest bank? | Could you tell me where the nearest bank **is**? |
| I don't know what does she want. | I don't know what **she wants**. |
| Who did call you? | **Who called you?** *("who" is already the subject)* |
| Do you know is she coming? | Do you know **if/whether** she is coming? |
| She asked me where do I live. | She asked me where **I live**. |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fix: "Can you tell me where is the station?"</strong></summary>

**Can you tell me where the station is?** — embedded questions keep statement word order.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Which is correct: "Who did break the window?" or "Who broke the window?"</strong></summary>

**"Who broke the window?"** — "who" is the subject here, so no auxiliary is needed.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Turn into an indirect question: "Is the library open on Sundays?"</strong></summary>

**I wonder if/whether the library is open on Sundays.** — yes/no questions need "if" or "whether" when embedded.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Fix: "I don't know what time does the train leave."</strong></summary>

**I don't know what time the train leaves.** — statement word order, no "does."
</details>
