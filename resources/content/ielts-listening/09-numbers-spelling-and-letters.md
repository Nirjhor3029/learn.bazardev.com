# Chapter 9 — Numbers, Spelling, and Letters: Getting the Details Exactly Right

> **Course:** IELTS Listening · **Chapter:** 9 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/09-numbers-spelling-and-letters](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/09-numbers-spelling-and-letters)

Across every question type in this course, the single most common way to lose an otherwise-correct mark is a small detail: a spelling slip, a misheard digit, a wrong currency symbol. This chapter is entirely about avoiding those losses.

Writing the numeral instead of spelling out the word avoids spelling mistakes entirely, and both forms are accepted. For phone numbers and codes, listen carefully for "double" (repeat the digit) and "oh" (zero).

## 2. Currency and unit symbols matter

If the recording says "pounds" and you write "$", the answer is marked wrong — the symbol itself carries meaning. Match the exact currency or unit mentioned, not a default you're used to.

## 3. Commonly misspelled words

| Word | Common mistake |
|---|---|
| **autumn** | Dropping the silent "n" at the end |
| **February** | Dropping the first "r" |
| **Wednesday** | Dropping the silent "d" |

These words are deliberately common in IELTS Listening precisely because they're easy to misspell under time pressure. Practising their spelling ahead of time removes an easy, preventable loss.

## 4. Confusable letters when spelling is given aloud

Just like Chapter 2's form-completion names, letters can be misheard when spelled aloud: B/V, B/P/D, and M/N are the most frequent mix-ups. If a name or code is spelled letter by letter, listen for the exact sound of each one rather than guessing from the first letter or two.

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Writing out "thirty" instead of "30," risking a spelling slip. | Write numerals — they're accepted and avoid spelling errors. |
| Using the wrong currency symbol out of habit. | Match the exact currency/unit mentioned in the recording. |
| Losing a mark on a well-known tricky word like "Wednesday." | Practise commonly misspelled words ahead of time. |
| Guessing a spelled-out letter from a similar sound. | Listen carefully for each letter — B/V, B/P/D, M/N are easy to confuse. |

## 6. Quick practice
