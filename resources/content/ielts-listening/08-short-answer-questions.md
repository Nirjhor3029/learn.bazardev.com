# Chapter 8 — Short-Answer Questions

> **Course:** IELTS Listening · **Chapter:** 8 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/08-short-answer-questions](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/08-short-answer-questions)

Short-answer questions ask a direct question — "What time...?", "How much...?", "Where...?" — and want a brief, factual answer taken from the recording: a place, a price, a time, a name. They test whether you can catch one concrete fact inside a stream of speech.

Reading the question first and predicting the answer type (time, place, price, name) narrows what you listen for, the same prediction skill from Chapter 1, applied here to a direct question instead of a gap.

## 2. Spelling and word limits still apply

A correct answer with a spelling mistake still loses the mark, and exceeding the stated word limit scores zero — exactly the same rules from Chapters 2 and 7.

## 3. These questions target concrete, everyday facts

Short-answer questions are most common on everyday, practical topics — prices, opening times, addresses, phone numbers, names — rather than abstract academic ideas. Prepare by getting comfortable with how these get said aloud in natural speech (Chapter 9 covers numbers and spelling in depth).

## 4. Expect the question's wording to be paraphrased in the audio

Just like sentence completion, the question itself is usually a paraphrase of how the audio actually introduces the information — don't wait to hear the question's exact words repeated.

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Not predicting the answer type before listening. | Read the question first; predict whether you need a time, place, price, or name. |
| Writing the answer with an incorrect spelling. | Spelling must be correct — a misheard or misspelled correct answer still loses the mark. |
| Expecting the question's exact wording in the audio. | Expect paraphrase — the audio introduces the fact differently than the question states it. |
| Ignoring the word limit when an answer "feels" longer. | Respect the stated limit exactly, just like every other completion-style question. |
