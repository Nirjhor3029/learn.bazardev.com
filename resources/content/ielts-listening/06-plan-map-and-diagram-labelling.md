# Chapter 6 — Plan, Map, and Diagram Labelling

> **Course:** IELTS Listening · **Chapter:** 6 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/06-plan-map-and-diagram-labelling](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/06-plan-map-and-diagram-labelling)

Here you're given a floor plan, map, or diagram with locations marked by letters (A, B, C...), and you match each spoken description to the correct letter. Unlike most other Listening types, you're usually **choosing a letter**, not writing a word — so word limits rarely matter here. What matters is tracking position as the speaker talks.

The speaker always has to establish where they're starting from ("if you come in through the main entrance...") — catch this first, or the whole route that follows won't make sense.

## 2. Build a mini-compass if directions matter

Unless the plan shows its own compass rose, assume **north = top, east = right** on most IELTS maps. Lightly mark the four directions in a corner before listening if the task uses compass language (north, south, east, west, and the points between).

## 3. Listen for position and direction language

| For maps/plans | For diagrams |
|---|---|
| "turn left/right," "go straight ahead" | "this part," "the segment that," "the bit which" |
| "next to," "opposite," "between," "behind" | "on the left-hand side," "at the top," "in the middle" |
| "the second door/turning on the right" | "where the two lines meet" |

## 4. Follow the route in real time — don't jump ahead

As the speaker describes a route, trace it with your pencil as you listen. If you lose the thread for a second, pick up the next direction word ("then," "after that," "next") and rejoin the route — don't try to reconstruct the whole path from memory afterward.

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Not identifying the starting point before the route begins. | Catch the starting point first — the whole route is described relative to it. |
| Assuming north is always at the top without checking. | Check for a compass rose on the plan; only assume north = top if none is shown. |
| Trying to reconstruct the whole route from memory after listening. | Trace the route in real time with your pencil as the speaker talks. |
| Expecting a written-word answer here, with a word limit to respect. | You're usually selecting a letter — word limits generally don't apply. |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Why is it important to catch the starting point before the route begins?</strong></summary>

**The whole route is described relative to it** — missing it makes the rest of the directions hard to follow.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. The plan has no compass rose. Where should you assume north is?</strong></summary>

**At the top** — the default assumption for most IELTS maps, with east to the right.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. You lose track of the route for a moment. What should you do?</strong></summary>

**Pick up the next direction word** ("then," "after that") and rejoin — don't try to reconstruct it from memory afterward.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Do word-limit rules usually apply to plan/map/diagram labelling answers?</strong></summary>

**Generally no** — you're usually choosing a letter corresponding to a location, not writing a word.
</details>
