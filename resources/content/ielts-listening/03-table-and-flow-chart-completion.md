Flow-chart gaps correspond to the arrows on the page. Listen for sequencing language — "after that," "once this is done," "the next step is" — to catch exactly when the speaker reaches the gap you need.

## 3. If you miss a gap, guess from position and grammar

Can't recall what was said for one gap? Use its position in the table/flow-chart and its grammar (is it a noun slot? a number slot?) to make an educated, logical guess rather than leaving it blank — remember, there's no penalty for a wrong guess.

## 4. Spelling: UK and US spelling are both accepted

Unlike some strict rules elsewhere, both British and American spellings count as correct for table and flow-chart answers (e.g., "colour"/"color"). The word limit and exact-wording rules from Chapter 2 still apply otherwise.

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Assuming a table's gaps can appear in any order. | The audio moves top-to-bottom, left-to-right — matching the table's layout. |
| Missing the gap because you didn't catch the sequencing word. | Listen actively for "after that," "once," "next" to locate the right step. |
| Leaving a missed gap blank. | Guess from position and grammar — no penalty for a wrong guess. |
| Worrying about UK vs US spelling. | Both are accepted for table and flow-chart completion. |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. In a table-completion task, does the audio describe the rows in any random order?</strong></summary>

**No** — it moves top-to-bottom, left-to-right, matching the table's own layout.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. What kind of language helps you locate a flow-chart gap?</strong></summary>

**Sequencing words** like "after that," "once this is done," or "the next step is."
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. You miss a gap entirely. What should you do?</strong></summary>

**Guess from its position and grammar** — there's no penalty for a wrong answer.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Does "colour" vs "color" matter for a table-completion answer?</strong></summary>

**No** — both UK and US spellings are accepted.
</details>
