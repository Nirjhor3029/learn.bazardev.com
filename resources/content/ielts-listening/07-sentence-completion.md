# Chapter 7 — Sentence Completion

> **Course:** IELTS Listening · **Chapter:** 7 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/07-sentence-completion](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/07-sentence-completion)

Sentence completion gives you a gapped sentence and asks you to fill it with words taken from the recording. It's Listening's version of Reading's completion tasks — same word-limit discipline, now under time pressure with no replay.

"Originally built" is paraphrased as "when it was first constructed" — don't expect to hear the sentence's exact wording. The answer itself ("warehouse") is usually copied directly from the recording, just like Reading's short-answer questions.

## 2. The word-limit rule, with two extra details

- Hyphenated words (like "police-man") count as **one** word.
- Contractions are never tested as an answer.
- A typical instruction looks like "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" — a number counts as its own unit, separate from the word count.

## 3. Watch for signal words

Phrases that mark key information or a shift in topic — "the main reason," "what's interesting is," "on the other hand" — often sit right next to the answer. They're worth tuning your ear to, the same way sequencing words help with flow-charts.

## 4. Answers follow the recording's order

Just like every other completion type in this course, sentence completion gaps appear in the same order as the information in the recording. If you're unsure about one, move to the next rather than waiting.

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Expecting the sentence's exact wording in the audio. | Expect paraphrase — the sentence restates the idea differently. |
| Writing a hyphenated word as if it were two words for the limit. | Hyphenated words count as **one** word. |
| Missing the answer because you didn't catch a signal word nearby. | Tune in to signal phrases like "the main reason" or "what's interesting is." |
| Assuming gaps can appear in any order in the recording. | Gaps follow the recording's order, just like every other completion type. |
