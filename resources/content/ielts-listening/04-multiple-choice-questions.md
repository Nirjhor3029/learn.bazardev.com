# Chapter 4 — Multiple Choice Questions

> **Course:** IELTS Listening · **Chapter:** 4 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/04-multiple-choice-questions](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/04-multiple-choice-questions)

Multiple choice appears in almost every Listening test, in two formats: pick **one** correct answer from three options, or pick **two or three** correct answers from a longer list. Both formats share the same trap.

Speakers frequently raise two wrong options first — using words that match the wrong answer choices almost exactly — before landing on the real answer, often with a correction signal ("but," "actually," "in the end"). If you pick the first option you hear mentioned, you'll often be wrong.

## 2. Before listening: read every option, don't just skim

With multiple choice, you can't predict a single word the way you can with completion gaps — instead, read **all** the options for a question before the audio starts, and underline each option's key difference from the others. This tells you exactly what distinction to listen for.

## 3. Match the time signal

Check whether the question asks about the past, present, or future — "What did the speaker originally plan?" versus "What will the speaker do now?" need different parts of the audio, and a changed-plan statement is a common way this question type tests careful listening.

## 4. Choose-two-or-three: keep tracking after your first match

In the multi-answer format, don't stop listening once you've found one correct option — the others are often scattered further along in the same section, not grouped together.

## 5. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Choosing the option whose exact words you hear first. | Keep listening — that option is often rejected a moment later. |
| Skimming the options instead of reading all of them carefully. | Read every option before listening and note what distinguishes them. |
| Ignoring whether the question asks about the past, present, or future. | Match the time signal in the question to the right part of the audio. |
| Stopping after finding one answer in a choose-multiple question. | Keep listening — the remaining correct options are often spread out. |

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. A speaker mentions "cotton," then "nylon," then says "in the end, we chose polyester." Which is the answer?</strong></summary>

**Polyester** — the correct option is often mentioned last, after the others are raised and rejected.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. Why should you read every answer option before the audio starts?</strong></summary>

**To know exactly what distinguishes each option**, since you can't predict a single word the way you can with completion gaps.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. The question asks "What will the speaker do now?" Which part of the audio matters most?</strong></summary>

**The speaker's current/future plan**, not an earlier, now-changed plan — match the question's time signal.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. In a choose-two-or-three question, you've found one correct option. What should you do?</strong></summary>

**Keep listening** — the remaining correct options are often scattered further along, not grouped together.
</details>
