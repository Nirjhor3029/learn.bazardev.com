## 2. One trap per chapter — the master list

| # | Topic | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| [1](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/01-how-ielts-listening-is-scored) | Scoring/overview | Leaving a gap blank when unsure. | There's no negative marking — always write a guess. |
| [2](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/02-form-and-note-completion) | Form/note completion | Waiting passively for the gap's answer to appear. | Turn each gap into a question before listening. |
| [3](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/03-table-and-flow-chart-completion) | Table/flow-chart | Assuming a table's gaps can appear in any order. | The audio follows the table's layout, top-to-bottom, left-to-right. |
| [4](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/04-multiple-choice-questions) | Multiple choice | Choosing the option whose exact words you hear first. | The correct option is often mentioned **last**, after others are rejected. |
| [5](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/05-matching) | Matching | Listening for the options' exact printed words. | Listen for paraphrase — the recording rarely repeats an option's wording. |
| [6](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/06-plan-map-and-diagram-labelling) | Plan/map/diagram | Not identifying the starting point before a route begins. | Catch the starting point first — the whole route is relative to it. |
| [7](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/07-sentence-completion) | Sentence completion | Expecting the sentence's exact wording in the audio. | Expect paraphrase; remember hyphenated words count as one word. |
| [8](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/08-short-answer-questions) | Short-answer | Not predicting the answer type before listening. | Predict a time, place, price, or name before the audio starts. |
| [9](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/09-numbers-spelling-and-letters) | Numbers/spelling | Writing out number words, risking a spelling slip. | Write numerals — "$30," not "thirty dollars." |
| [10](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/10-distractors-and-the-changed-answer-trap) | Distractors | Writing down the first fact or number you hear. | Wait for the full sentence — a correction often follows. |
| [11](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/11-accents-and-signposting-language) | Accents/pacing | Freezing after a missed question, losing the next ones too. | Let a miss go immediately — the audio doesn't pause. |

## 3. The combined pre-test checklist

**Before each section starts:**
1. Did you use the pause to **read every question** and predict word types (Chapter 2, 8)?
2. Do you know the **starting point** for any plan/map question (Chapter 6)?

**While listening:**
3. Are you expecting **paraphrase**, not exact words, for matching and completion types (Chapter 5, 7)?
4. Are you watching for **self-corrections** and hidden negatives, not just the first thing said (Chapter 4, 10)?
5. For multiple choice, are you waiting to hear the **final, corrected** answer (Chapter 4)?
6. For numbers, are you writing **numerals**, with the correct currency/unit (Chapter 9)?

**If something goes wrong:**
7. Did you miss a question? **Let it go immediately** and listen for the next cue (Chapter 11).
8. Is a gap genuinely missed at the end? **Guess** from position and grammar — there's no penalty (Chapter 3).

**Before submitting:**
9. Did you check spelling on every written answer, including tricky words like "February" (Chapter 9)?
10. Did every single question get an answer, even a guess (Chapter 1)?

## 4. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Which single habit prevents the most avoidable losses across every question type?</strong></summary>

**Always writing an answer, even a guess** — there's no negative marking, so a blank is the only guaranteed zero.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. A multiple choice option's exact wording is spoken early in the audio. Should you choose it immediately?</strong></summary>

**No** — wait, since the correct option is often mentioned last, after others are raised and rejected.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which theme covers mistakes like exceeding the word limit or writing numbers as words?</strong></summary>

**Word-level precision** — the theme behind Chapters 7 and 9's mistakes.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Which single skill connects matching, sentence completion, and short-answer questions most directly?</strong></summary>

**Recognising paraphrase** — expecting different wording for the same meaning, not an exact match.
</details>
