# Chapter 10 — Distractors and the Changed-Answer Trap

Chapters 1 and 4 already introduced self-correction. This chapter generalises it: IELTS Listening audio is **deliberately written** to mention wrong information before the right answer, across every question type in this course. Recognising the pattern is a skill in itself.

## 2. The three trap types, named

| Trap | What it looks like | How to catch it |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-correction** | "At three — oh sorry, actually four o'clock." | Always take the **final, corrected** statement as the answer. |
| **Hidden negative** | "We don't serve food," "you can't avoid the fee." | Negative words ("don't," "never," "avoid") are easy to miss — listen for them actively, since IELTS rarely uses a loud, explicit "NOT." |
| **Rejected option** | "Bring a packed lunch, not snacks." | A word you recognise from the options might be the one being **ruled out**, not confirmed. |

## 3. Don't trust keyword matches alone

If an answer option shares an exact word with something you just heard, pause before choosing it — the speaker may have used that word specifically to reject it, with the real answer following moments later. This is the same lesson from Chapter 4's multiple choice, now applied everywhere.

## 4. Ignore facts that don't answer the actual question

Recordings often include extra numbers, names, or details that sound important but don't answer the specific question being asked — these exist to test whether you're tracking the right piece of information, not just any information.

## 5. Only use what's actually said — never outside knowledge

Just like Reading's golden rule, never fill in an answer based on what you'd expect to be true in real life. If the recording doesn't state it, it isn't the answer, no matter how logical it seems.

## 6. Common mistakes to fix

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| Writing down the first number or fact you hear. | Wait for the full sentence — a correction often follows. |
| Missing a hidden negative like "don't" or "never." | Actively listen for negative words — they're rarely emphasised. |
| Choosing an option because you recognise its exact wording. | That wording may be the one thing being rejected — listen for what follows. |
| Filling a gap with outside knowledge that "sounds right." | Only use what the recording actually states. |

## 7. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. "The tour starts at 10am — wait no, make that 10:30." What's the correct time?</strong></summary>

**10:30** — the corrected, final statement is always the answer.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "We don't serve food" is an example of which trap?</strong></summary>

**A hidden negative** — easy to miss since "don't" often isn't emphasised.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. "Bring a packed lunch, not snacks." Which word is the distractor?</strong></summary>

**"Snacks"** — it's mentioned only to be rejected; "packed lunch" is correct.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. Should you ever fill in an answer based on outside knowledge, if the recording doesn't state it?</strong></summary>

**No** — only use what's actually said in the recording, regardless of how logical an unstated answer seems.
</details>
