# Chapter 1 — How IELTS Listening Is Scored, and the One Skill That Matters Most

> **Course:** IELTS Listening · **Chapter:** 1 of 12
> Read on the website: [learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/01-how-ielts-listening-is-scored](https://learn.bazardev.com/ielts-listening/01-how-ielts-listening-is-scored)

Reading lets you re-read a passage as many times as you like. Listening gives you **one pass** — the recording plays once, and once it's gone, you can't get it back. Every strategy in this course exists to make that one pass count.

Your raw score (0-40) converts to the familiar 9-band scale. Since nothing is deducted for a wrong answer, **every question should get an answer**, even a guess, before each section ends.

## 2. The four sections get harder as you go

| Section | What you'll hear | Question style |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | A conversation about an everyday, practical topic (e.g., booking a hotel room) | Usually form/note completion |
| **2** | A monologue on an everyday topic (e.g., a guide describing a museum) | Often maps, plans, or multiple choice |
| **3** | A conversation between up to four people in an academic/training context | Mixed question types, more opinion and discussion |
| **4** | An academic lecture-style monologue, one speaker | Usually summary/note completion — the hardest section |

Because you only get one pass, reading the questions **before** the audio reaches them is critical — you get a short pause for exactly this purpose. Two things to do in that pause:

1. **Predict the word type.** Is the gap a number, a name, a place, a single word? This narrows what you listen for before you even hear it.
2. **Expect paraphrase, not the exact word.** Just like Reading, if the question says "important," the recording will likely use a different word for the same idea — don't wait to hear the question's exact wording.

And while listening, watch for **self-correction** — speakers often say one answer, then change it ("Platform three — oh wait, sorry, Platform five"). The corrected, final answer is always the one that counts.

## 4. The golden rule: write exactly what you hear, spelling included

Just like Reading completion questions, your answer must respect the **word limit** given in the instructions, and spelling must be correct — a misheard or misspelled correct answer still loses the mark. Chapters 7 and 9 cover numbers, letters, and spelling traps in depth.

## 5. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. How many times does the recording play?</strong></summary>

**Once** — there's no replay, which is why reading ahead during the pause matters so much.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. A speaker says "Platform three — oh sorry, I mean Platform five." What's the correct answer?</strong></summary>

**5** — the corrected, final answer always counts, not the first one mentioned.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which section is generally considered the hardest?</strong></summary>

**Section 4** — a single-speaker academic lecture with no conversation to help confirm details.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. How many marks do you lose for a wrong guess?</strong></summary>

**Zero** — there's no negative marking, so always answer every question.
</details>
