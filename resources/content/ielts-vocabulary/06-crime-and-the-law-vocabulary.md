## 3. High-value collocations, grouped by theme

**Crime and offences**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **commit a crime** | *Most people who commit a crime are caught within a year.* |
| **petty crime** | *Petty crime, such as shoplifting, has risen in city centres.* |
| **juvenile delinquency** | *Juvenile delinquency is often linked to a lack of parental supervision.* |
| **a repeat offender** | *Repeat offenders are far more likely to reoffend without proper support.* |
| **act as a deterrent** | *Supporters argue that harsh sentences act as a deterrent to crime.* |

**Law enforcement and justice**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **enforce the law** | *It is the police's responsibility to enforce the law fairly.* |
| **bring someone to justice** | *Investigators worked for years to bring the fraudster to justice.* |
| **a fair trial** | *Every defendant has the right to a fair trial.* |
| **impose a sentence** | *The judge imposed a ten-year sentence for the offence.* |
| **capital punishment** | *Countries remain divided over whether capital punishment should exist.* |

**Punishment and rehabilitation**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **serve a sentence** | *He served a five-year sentence before being released.* |
| **community service** | *Instead of prison, she was ordered to complete community service.* |
| **reintegrate into society** | *Rehabilitation programmes help former prisoners reintegrate into society.* |
| **reduce recidivism** | *Education programmes in prisons have been shown to reduce recidivism.* |

## 4. Contrast pairs worth knowing

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| petty crime vs. violent crime | minor offences (theft, vandalism) vs. serious offences involving harm |
| punishment vs. rehabilitation | penalising the offender vs. helping them reform and reintegrate |
| civil law vs. criminal law | disputes between individuals/organisations vs. offences against the state |

## 5. Seeing it all combined (active use)

*"While some argue that harsh sentences act as a deterrent and that offenders who commit a crime must serve a sentence as punishment, others believe rehabilitation programmes that help prisoners reintegrate into society are more effective at reducing recidivism."*

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fill in: "He was found guilty and received a criminal ___." (convict / conviction / convicted)</strong></summary>

**conviction** — the noun form fits after "received a criminal".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "Supporters argue that harsh sentences act as a ___ to crime." (deterrent / detergent)</strong></summary>

**deterrent** — something that discourages an action.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which pair contrasts minor offences with serious offences involving harm?</strong></summary>

**Petty crime vs. violent crime.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. "Education programmes in prisons have been shown to reduce ___." (recidivism / conviction)</strong></summary>

**recidivism** — the tendency of a convicted criminal to reoffend.
</details>
