## 3. High-value collocations, grouped by theme

**The scientific method**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **conduct an experiment** | *Researchers conducted a controlled experiment over six months.* |
| **draw a conclusion** | *It's too early to draw a conclusion from such a small sample.* |
| **empirical evidence** | *The theory is now backed by strong empirical evidence.* |
| **peer-reviewed research** | *The findings were published in a peer-reviewed journal.* |
| **a groundbreaking discovery** | *The team made a groundbreaking discovery in cancer treatment.* |

**Funding and application**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **a scientific breakthrough** | *The vaccine was hailed as a major scientific breakthrough.* |
| **secure research funding** | *Universities often struggle to secure research funding.* |
| **practical applications** | *The discovery has several practical applications in agriculture.* |
| **cutting-edge research** | *The lab is known for its cutting-edge research in genetics.* |

**Risks and ethics**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **genetic engineering** | *Advances in genetic engineering have raised new possibilities in medicine.* |
| **ethical concerns** | *Gene editing in humans raises serious ethical concerns.* |
| **unintended consequences** | *Scientists warn of the unintended consequences of releasing modified organisms.* |
| **space exploration** | *Investment in space exploration has increased significantly in recent decades.* |

## 4. Contrast pairs worth knowing

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| pure science vs. applied science | research for its own sake vs. research aimed at practical use |
| qualitative vs. quantitative research | descriptive, non-numerical data vs. numerical, measurable data |
| correlation vs. causation | two things occurring together vs. one directly causing the other |

## 5. Seeing it all combined (active use)

*"While the groundbreaking discovery was based on strong empirical evidence and peer-reviewed research, critics have raised ethical concerns about the unintended consequences of applying genetic engineering before its long-term effects are fully understood."*

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fill in: "The team published a detailed ___ of the results." (analyze / analysis / analytical)</strong></summary>

**analysis** — the noun form fits after "a detailed".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "It's too early to ___ a conclusion from such a small sample." (draw / write)</strong></summary>

**draw** — "draw a conclusion" is the fixed collocation.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which pair contrasts two things occurring together with one directly causing the other?</strong></summary>

**Correlation vs. causation.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. "Universities often struggle to secure research ___." (funding / fundament)</strong></summary>

**funding** — "secure research funding" is the standard collocation.
</details>
