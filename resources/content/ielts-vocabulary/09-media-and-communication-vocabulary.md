## 3. High-value collocations, grouped by theme

**News and journalism**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **breaking news** | *The channel interrupted its programme for breaking news.* |
| **media bias** | *Critics have accused the network of media bias.* |
| **freedom of the press** | *Freedom of the press is considered essential to a healthy democracy.* |
| **investigative journalism** | *Investigative journalism uncovered the corruption scandal.* |
| **the spread of fake news** | *Social media has accelerated the spread of fake news.* |

**Social media and advertising**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **go viral** | *The video went viral within hours of being posted.* |
| **a social media platform** | *The company launched its own social media platform.* |
| **targeted advertising** | *Targeted advertising relies heavily on users' personal data.* |
| **raise brand awareness** | *Influencer campaigns are often used to raise brand awareness.* |
| **influencer marketing** | *Influencer marketing has grown rapidly over the past decade.* |

**Effects and concerns**

| Collocation | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| **an echo chamber** | *Social media algorithms can trap users in an echo chamber of similar opinions.* |
| **invasion of privacy** | *Many users view targeted ads as an invasion of privacy.* |
| **desensitise viewers** | *Constant exposure to violent content can desensitise viewers.* |
| **mass media** | *The mass media plays a powerful role in shaping public opinion.* |

## 4. Contrast pairs worth knowing

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| traditional media vs. social media | newspapers/TV/radio vs. internet-based platforms |
| censorship vs. freedom of the press | restricting information vs. the right to report freely |
| objective vs. biased reporting | presenting facts neutrally vs. favouring one viewpoint |

## 5. Seeing it all combined (active use)

*"While social media platforms have made it easier to raise brand awareness and connect people across the world, critics warn that the spread of fake news and the echo chamber effect pose a serious risk to objective public debate."*

## 6. Quick practice

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. Fill in: "The documentary was very ___ about climate change." (inform / information / informative)</strong></summary>

**informative** — the adjective form fits after "very".
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. "The video ___ within hours of being posted." (went viral / went viewed)</strong></summary>

**went viral** — meaning it spread rapidly online.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. Which pair contrasts restricting information with the right to report freely?</strong></summary>

**Censorship vs. freedom of the press.**
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. "Social media algorithms can trap users in an ___ of similar opinions." (echo chamber / breaking news)</strong></summary>

**echo chamber** — an environment where one only encounters opinions similar to one's own.
</details>
